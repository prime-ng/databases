# Trip and Discipline Report

## What This Report Shows

Mrs. Desai, the Transport Supervisor at Green Valley School, uses this report every morning to understand what happened during the previous day's transport operations. The report gives her a complete picture of all trips that were scheduled, which trips actually ran, whether they departed and arrived on time, how many students were picked up and dropped off, and most importantly, whether there were any safety concerns flagged during those trips.

This report connects trip-level execution data with student boarding records. Mrs. Desai can see at a glance which routes performed well, which drivers followed the schedule, and where delays or safety issues occurred. The discipline aspect comes from tracking whether students boarded at their designated stops and whether any unexpected behavior or incidents were logged against a particular trip.

The report is designed to answer questions like: Did all scheduled trips run yesterday? Which trips had delays and how severe were they? Were there any safety flags raised? How many students were actually transported compared to what was planned? Which routes have the best and worst on-time performance? Are there patterns of repeated delays on certain routes or with certain drivers?

Mrs. Desai values this report because it shifts transport management from reactive to proactive. Instead of waiting for a parent complaint about a late pickup or a missed drop-off, she can review the report each morning and address issues before they escalate. The school principal also receives a summary version of this report during the weekly management meeting to review transport reliability.

## Default Data Load / Filters

When Mrs. Desai opens the Trip and Discipline Report, the system automatically loads data for the current academic session and the last seven days including today. This gives her a rolling week view that covers the most recent complete school days. The default view shows all routes combined, all drivers, all vehicles, and every trip type including pickup, drop-off, and special trips.

The report provides the following filter options:

Date Range filter allows Mrs. Desai to select any custom start and end date. She commonly uses this at the end of each month to generate the monthly transport performance summary for the school administration. The date range filter defaults to a seven-day window but can be expanded to cover a full term or reduced to a single day for detailed investigation.

Route filter lists every active route configured in the system. Mrs. Desai can select one specific route to see only the trips belonging to that route, or select multiple routes to compare their performance side by side. The filter shows route names as they are known in the school, such as "Route 7A - Tilak Nagar" or "Route 12B - Dwarka Sector 14."

Driver filter lists all drivers who have been assigned trips during the selected date range. Mrs. Desai uses this filter when she wants to review the performance of a specific driver, especially after receiving feedback or complaints from parents. The filter shows driver names along with their employee codes for accurate identification.

Vehicle filter lists all vehicles that were used for trips in the selected period. Mrs. Desai uses this filter when investigating a specific vehicle's performance, particularly if there have been maintenance issues or if a vehicle was replaced at short notice. The filter displays vehicle registration numbers.

All filters can be used together or separately. When multiple filters are applied, the report data narrows down to show only the trips that match every selected filter condition. Mrs. Desai can also reset all filters to their default state with a single click.

## When This Report Is Used

The Trip and Discipline Report is used at multiple points throughout the school day and week. Mrs. Desai checks it first thing in the morning, around 7:30 AM, right after the first batch of pickup trips have completed their runs. She looks at the previous day's completion rate and any overnight safety flags that may have been raised by drivers reporting issues after their last drop-off.

During the mid-morning, around 10:00 AM, the transport team meets briefly to review the report. The team discusses any trips that showed delays of more than fifteen minutes and plans corrective actions. If a particular route consistently shows delays, Mrs. Desai may decide to adjust the route schedule or reassign the driver.

At the end of each week, typically on Friday afternoon, Mrs. Desai generates the weekly summary version of this report. She compares week-over-week trends in completion rate and average delay. If she sees a decline in performance, she investigates the root cause and schedules retraining or route optimization sessions during the weekend.

The report is also used during parent-teacher meetings when a parent raises a transport-related concern. Mrs. Desai can pull up the specific trip and date to show the parent exactly what happened, whether the child boarded on time, and whether there were any delays or incidents. This transparency has significantly reduced disputes and improved parent satisfaction.

At the end of every academic term, the school administration uses the aggregated data from this report to evaluate the overall transport department performance. The principal reviews the average on-time performance percentage and the safety incident rate before approving transport budget allocations for the next term.

## Key Metrics / KPIs

Total Trips shows the count of all trips that were scheduled within the selected date range and filter criteria. This includes both completed trips and any trips that were cancelled or not executed. Mrs. Desai uses this number to understand the volume of transport operations she is managing. A typical school day at Green Valley involves between 40 and 55 trips depending on the number of active routes and the special activity schedule.

Planned Boardings refers to the total number of student boarding events that were scheduled to happen across all trips in the selected period. This is calculated based on the route schedules and the students allocated to each stop. For example, if a route has ten stops and three students are scheduled to board at each stop, that route contributes thirty planned boardings per trip. Mrs. Desai reviews this number to understand the expected transport load.

Actual Boardings counts the total number of students who were physically marked as boarded during the trips. This data comes from the driver or attendant recording each student as they enter or leave the vehicle. If a student was absent on a given day, they would not be marked as boarded, which means the actual boarding count will be lower than the planned count. Mrs. Desai compares planned versus actual to identify attendance patterns and also to verify that drivers are correctly recording every student.

Safety Status is a critical metric that classifies each trip as SAFE or RISK. A trip receives SAFE status when no safety-related incidents were reported during the trip, the vehicle completed its route without mechanical issues, and the driver followed all prescribed safety protocols. A trip receives RISK status if any incident was reported, such as a student injury, a near-miss traffic event, a vehicle breakdown, or a behavioural issue that required intervention. Mrs. Desai investigates every RISK trip personally within twenty-four hours.

Completion Rate is the percentage of scheduled trips that were actually executed and completed without being cancelled or aborted midway. A completion rate of one hundred percent means every planned trip ran from start to finish. Mrs. Desai targets a minimum completion rate of ninety-eight percent. Any drop below this threshold triggers an immediate review of the reasons for non-completion, which could include driver absenteeism, vehicle breakdown, or severe weather conditions.

Average Delay measures the average number of minutes by which trips deviated from their scheduled departure or arrival times. A positive value indicates trips were delayed on average, while a zero or negative value indicates trips ran on time or early. Mrs. Desai considers delays under five minutes as acceptable. Delays between five and fifteen minutes require attention, and delays beyond fifteen minutes require formal reporting. The report also highlights the specific trips with the highest delays so Mrs. Desai can investigate individually.

## Charts and Visualizations

The report contains two primary charts that help Mrs. Desai quickly absorb the operational picture without reading through rows of data.

The first chart is a Doughnut chart that shows the distribution of trip statuses across the selected period. This chart uses coloured segments to represent the proportion of trips in each status category. The system categorises trips into statuses such as Completed On Time, Completed Delayed, Cancelled, or Aborted. Mrs. Desai looks at this doughnut chart every morning to get an immediate sense of how the previous day went. If the Completed On Time segment is significantly smaller than the Completed Delayed segment, she knows there is a systemic issue that needs investigation. The doughnut chart also shows exact percentages and counts in a tooltip when she hovers over each segment.

The second chart is a Bar chart that displays route-wise completion rate. Each bar represents one route, and the height of the bar shows the completion percentage for that route over the selected period. The bars are colour-coded. Green bars indicate routes with completion rates above the ninety-eight percent target. Yellow bars indicate routes between ninety and ninety-eight percent. Red bars indicate routes below ninety percent completion. Mrs. Desai uses this bar chart during her weekly team meeting to highlight which routes need improvement. She can click on any bar to drill down and see the individual trips for that route.

Both charts update automatically when filters are changed. If Mrs. Desai selects a specific driver from the filter, the charts immediately redraw to show only that driver's trips. This interactivity allows her to explore the data without needing to generate multiple separate reports.

## Data Sources

The report pulls data from several connected tables in the transport module. These tables work together to provide a complete picture of each trip and its associated activities.

The tpt_trip table is the central table that stores one record for every trip that is scheduled or executed. Each record contains the trip date, scheduled start time, actual start time, scheduled end time, actual end time, trip status, and route identifier. This table forms the backbone of the report because every other piece of information is linked to a specific trip record.

The tpt_route_scheduler_jnt table is referenced in the system as routeScheduler. This table defines the schedule pattern for routes, including which days of the week a route runs, the expected departure times, and the standard duration. The report uses this table to determine whether a trip was scheduled according to the standard pattern or was an ad hoc arrangement.

The tpt_route table stores the master list of all routes configured in the school transport system. Each route record includes the route name, description, total distance, estimated travel time, and the list of stops in sequence. The report uses this table to group trips by route and to display route names in filters and charts.

The tpt_vehicle table contains records for each vehicle in the school transport fleet. Vehicle details include registration number, vehicle type, seating capacity, and current operational status. The report uses this table to filter trips by vehicle and to display vehicle information alongside trip details.

The tpt_trip_stop_detail table is referenced in the system as tripStopDetail. This table records the planned stops for each trip, including the scheduled arrival time at each stop and the actual arrival time. The report uses this table to calculate whether trips arrived at each stop on time and to determine the overall trip delay based on cumulative stop-level deviations.

The tpt_personnel table is used to look up driver information. This table stores personnel records including name, employee code, contact number, licence details, and employment status. The report joins this table to the trip record to display the driver name for each trip and to enable filtering by driver.

The tpt_student_boarding_log table is referenced in the system as boardingLogs. This is the table where each individual student boarding event is recorded. When a student gets on or off the vehicle, the driver or attendant records the student identifier, the trip identifier, the stop where boarding occurred, and the timestamp. The report uses this table to calculate planned versus actual boardings and to generate the boarding completion metrics.

## Permissions

The Trip and Discipline Report is protected by the permission key tenant.trip-execution.viewAny. This permission is assigned to user roles that require access to transport execution data. The system checks this permission every time a user attempts to open the report. If the user's role does not include this permission, the report page shows an access denied message and the data is never loaded.

The permission is designed at the tenant level, which means that in a multi-school setup where Green Valley School is one tenant among several, the permission applies only within the boundaries of that tenant. A transport supervisor at one school cannot view trip data from another school even if they have the same role.

## Who Can Access

At Green Valley School, the following roles are granted the tenant.trip-execution.viewAny permission and can access the Trip and Discipline Report.

Mrs. Desai as the Transport Supervisor has full access to this report. She uses it as her primary tool for daily transport operations management. She can view all trips across all routes, drivers, and vehicles without any restriction.

The Transport Coordinator, who reports to Mrs. Desai, also has access to this report. The coordinator uses it to prepare the daily briefing and to follow up on specific trips that showed delays. The coordinator does not have permission to edit trip data but can view and export the report for sharing with the transport team.

The School Principal has view-only access to a summarized version of this report. The principal does not see every individual trip detail but can review the overall completion rate, safety status summary, and trend charts. This allows the principal to stay informed about transport performance without being overwhelmed by operational details.

The School Administrator, who handles parent communications, has access to this report so they can answer parent queries about specific trips and timings. When a parent calls to ask why their child reached home late, the administrator can look up the trip details and provide an accurate explanation.

Drivers and attendants do not have access to this report. They input data into the system during their trips, but the consolidated report is only visible to supervisory and management roles. This ensures that operational data is reviewed objectively by the management team.

## Example Scenario

It is Wednesday morning, and Mrs. Desai opens the Trip and Discipline Report at 7:45 AM. The default view shows the last seven days of data. She notices immediately that the doughnut chart has a noticeably smaller green segment compared to previous days. The completion rate for Tuesday is showing as ninety-four percent, which is below her ninety-eight percent target.

She applies the Route filter and selects Route 7A - Tilak Nagar, the route that serves the largest number of primary school students. The bar chart now shows that Route 7A has a completion rate of only eighty percent for Tuesday. Four out of the five scheduled trips for this route were completed, but one trip was marked as cancelled.

Mrs. Desai drills into the cancelled trip by clicking on the bar. She sees that the trip scheduled at 2:30 PM for the afternoon drop-off was cancelled. The system shows the reason for cancellation as Driver Not Available. She checks the driver filter and sees that the driver assigned to this trip, Mr. Sharma, was marked absent.

She then looks at the Average Delay metric. Among the four completed trips on Route 7A, the average delay was twelve minutes, which is in the attention zone. Two of the four trips had delays exceeding fifteen minutes, triggering the formal reporting threshold.

Mrs. Desai clicks on the Safety Status summary for Route 7A. All four completed trips show SAFE status, which is a relief. However, the low completion rate and the high average delay together tell her that Route 7A had a problematic day.

She exports the report data for Tuesday and sends an email to the Transport Coordinator asking them to speak with the backup driver who was assigned to cover Mr. Sharma's absence. She also schedules a meeting with Mr. Sharma for Thursday morning to discuss his absenteeism and to review whether there is a pattern of unplanned leaves.

The next morning, Mrs. Desai opens the report again. She sees that Route 7A's completion rate has recovered to one hundred percent for Wednesday. The average delay is down to four minutes. She notes this improvement and closes the report, satisfied that the intervention worked.

## Logic Flow

The Trip and Discipline Report follows a specific sequence of data processing steps to ensure accuracy and consistency.

The first step is the application of filters. When Mrs. Desai opens the report, the system takes the selected date range, route, driver, and vehicle filter values and uses them to narrow down the trip records from the tpt_trip table. Only trips that match all filter conditions are included in the subsequent calculations.

The second step is trip status classification. For each filtered trip record, the system examines the status field and the actual start and end times. A trip is classified as Completed On Time if the status indicates completion and the actual end time is within five minutes of the scheduled end time. A trip is classified as Completed Delayed if the status indicates completion but the actual end time exceeds the scheduled end time by more than five minutes. A trip is classified as Cancelled if the status field shows a cancelled value regardless of timing. A trip is classified as Aborted if the trip was started but could not be completed and was terminated midway.

The third step is boarding calculation. The system counts the number of student boarding records in the tpt_student_boarding_log table that are linked to the filtered trips. This count becomes the Actual Boardings figure. The Planned Boardings figure is calculated by counting the number of student-route-stop assignments that were active on the trip date. The system then compares these two numbers to derive a boarding completion percentage.

The fourth step is safety status determination. The system checks each trip for any incident records linked to it. If at least one incident exists, the trip is marked as RISK. If no incidents exist, the trip is marked as SAFE. The report then counts the number of SAFE and RISK trips and displays these as a summary metric.

The fifth step is delay calculation. For each completed trip, the system calculates the difference between the actual arrival time at the final stop and the scheduled arrival time at the final stop. This difference is expressed in minutes. The system then averages these differences across all completed trips to derive the Average Delay metric.

The sixth step is chart generation. The system takes the classified trip data and groups it by status for the doughnut chart. It also groups the data by route and calculates the completion rate per route for the bar chart. Both charts are rendered when the page loads.

The seventh step is aggregation. The system calculates the summary KPIs including Total Trips, Planned Boardings, Actual Boardings, Completion Rate, and Average Delay by applying the calculations described in the previous steps across all filtered data. These summary values are displayed at the top of the report page.

The eighth and final step is data presentation. The system displays the filtered and calculated data in a tabular format below the charts and KPIs. Each row in the table represents one trip and shows the trip date, route name, driver name, vehicle number, scheduled time, actual time, delay in minutes, status, safety status, planned boardings, and actual boardings. Mrs. Desai can sort the table by any column and can export the entire report to a PDF or spreadsheet file for sharing or record-keeping.

The report also maintains an audit trail. Every time Mrs. Desai or any authorised user opens the report, applies a filter, or exports data, the system logs this activity with the user name, timestamp, and the filter criteria used. This audit trail helps the school administration track who accessed transport performance data and for what purpose. In the event of a data discrepancy or a parent complaint about transport service, the audit trail provides a clear record of which reports were reviewed and when.

The data refresh schedule is an important part of the logic flow. The report does not show real-time data because trip data continues to be updated throughout the school day as trips complete and drivers submit their logs. Instead, the report refreshes every thirty minutes during school hours and performs a final nightly refresh at midnight. This means that when Mrs. Desai opens the report at seven forty-five in the morning, she is seeing data that was last refreshed at midnight, which includes all completed trips from the previous day. If she needs the absolute latest data during the day, she can click a manual refresh button that triggers an on-demand data reload.

The report also handles edge cases in its logic flow. If a trip has incomplete data, such as missing actual arrival times because the driver did not complete the trip log, the system excludes that trip from delay calculations but still includes it in the trip count. If a trip has boarding records that exceed the planned boarding count, perhaps because extra students were accommodated, the system uses the actual count as the primary figure and flags the discrepancy for Mrs. Desai's attention. These edge case handling rules ensure that the report remains accurate and useful even when real-world data is imperfect.

Another edge case involves cancelled trips that were replaced by backup trips. If a primary trip is cancelled but a substitute trip runs with a different vehicle and driver to cover the same route, the system treats these as two separate trip records. The cancelled trip is counted in the total but excluded from completion rate calculations. The substitute trip is evaluated independently for timeliness and safety. Mrs. Desai sees both records in the detailed table so she has a complete picture of what happened on that route for that day.

The system also accounts for split trips. On days when a single route requires multiple vehicles because of high student volume, such as during a school field trip or exam week when class timings are staggered, the system creates multiple trip records for the same route and schedule. The report aggregates these as separate trips and calculates metrics independently for each vehicle. Mrs. Desai can see at a glance whether both vehicles completed their runs on time or if one encountered delays while the other performed well.

A special consolidation feature in the logic flow allows Mrs. Desai to collapse multi-vehicle route data into a single route-level view. When she enables consolidation, the system combines all trips for a route into a single row showing aggregate metrics, the total number of vehicles used, the average delay across all vehicles, and the combined boarding counts. This helps her present a cleaner summary during management meetings without losing the confidence that comes from accurate underlying data.
