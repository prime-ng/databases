# Management Summary Report

## What This Report Shows

Mrs. Desai prepares the Management Summary Report for the school principal and the management committee at Green Valley School. This report is the executive overview of the entire transport department. It brings together the money that came in from student transport fees and the money that went out on fuel and vehicle maintenance. The report shows whether the transport department is running at a profit, at a loss, or breaking even. This is the single most important report for decision-making at the school leadership level. Mrs. Desai does not need to show the principal every minor expense or every individual student payment. Instead, this report summarises everything into a clear financial picture. The principal can look at this report and immediately understand how the transport department is performing financially. The report also shows trends over time. Mrs. Desai can compare this month revenue and costs against previous months to see if the financial health is improving or declining. This report is designed for readers who do not deal with transport operations daily. It avoids unnecessary detail and focuses on the big numbers that matter for budgeting and strategic planning. The Management Summary Report answers questions like: Did the school collect enough transport fees to cover fuel and maintenance costs? Are costs rising faster than revenue? Which month had the highest profit? Is the transport department a financial burden on the school or a self-sustaining operation? Should the school consider increasing transport fees for the next academic year? Is it financially viable to add a new route or purchase an additional vehicle? These strategic questions cannot be answered by looking at individual fuel receipts or fee slips. They require the consolidated view that only this report provides. The report also gives the principal confidence that the transport department is being managed responsibly. When the principal presents the school budget to the parent-teacher association, she can reference this report to show that transport fees are being used appropriately and that the department is not losing money. In essence, this report translates the day-to-day transport operations into a financial language that school leadership understands and trusts.

## Default Data Load / Filters

When Mrs. Desai opens the Management Summary Report, it automatically loads data for the current month. The date range is set from the first day of the current month to the last day of the current month. This gives a complete view of the current period without requiring any manual selection. Mrs. Desai can change the date range to look at a single month, a term, or a full academic year as needed. The date range filter uses a calendar picker with a start date field and an end date field. She clicks on each field and a calendar appears showing the days of the month. She can navigate between months using arrow buttons on the calendar. There is no vehicle filter in this report because the management summary is about the entire transport department, not individual vehicles. The report covers all routes, all students, and all vehicles together as one consolidated picture. This is intentional because the management team wants the big picture, not vehicle-level detail. They want to know the overall financial health of the transport department, not how much a specific bus costs. Mrs. Desai can apply the filter and the report updates every number and chart accordingly. There is an Apply button to refresh the data and a Reset button to go back to the current month default. The report also shows a small text line below the title indicating the period being viewed, such as April 2026 or Term 1 (April to June 2026), to avoid confusion about what data is being displayed. This text line updates automatically whenever the date range is changed.

## When This Report Is Used

The Management Summary Report is used on a regular and predictable schedule at Green Valley School. Mrs. Desai generates this report at the end of every month to attach to her monthly transport department report to the principal. The principal reviews it to ensure the department is not overspending. At the end of each academic term, Mrs. Desai prepares a more detailed version covering the full term for the school management committee meeting. During this meeting, the committee members ask questions about cost trends, revenue growth, and whether the transport fee structure needs revision. The report provides all the answers. During the annual budget meeting, the principal uses the full-year Management Summary Report to decide how much budget to allocate to the transport department for the next year. If the report shows a consistent loss across multiple terms, the management committee may decide to increase transport fees for the upcoming academic year. If the report shows a healthy profit, they may decide to invest in a new bus, improve the waiting area at pickup points, or increase driver salaries to retain good staff. This report is also used when the school applies for any accreditation or external audit, as it provides a clear financial statement of the transport operation. Accreditation bodies often ask to see how the school manages its transport finances, and this report serves as the official document. Sometimes Mrs. Desai uses this report to answer specific questions from the principal, such as whether the recent increase in diesel prices has affected the department profitability. In that case, she changes the date range to compare the months before and after the price increase. She can also use the report to calculate the average cost per student per month, which is a useful metric for parent communication. Overall, this report is the primary communication tool between Mrs. Desai and the school leadership regarding transport finances. Without this report, the principal would have to rely on scattered information from different sources, which would be inefficient and error-prone.

## Key Metrics / KPIs

The Management Summary Report presents five key numbers at the top in a summary bar. These numbers are carefully chosen to give a complete financial snapshot.

Total Routes shows the count of active routes during the selected date range. A route is considered active if at least one student was assigned to it during the period. This number gives the principal a sense of how many routes the school is operating. If the number of routes changes significantly from one term to the next, the principal can ask Mrs. Desai whether new routes were added or old ones were consolidated. This metric also helps the principal understand the scale of the transport operation. A school with 8 routes is running a moderately sized transport department, while a school with 20 routes would need significantly more vehicles, drivers, and coordination staff.

Total Boardings shows the total number of student boarding events recorded during the selected date range. Each time a student boards a vehicle, it is counted as one boarding. This is a measure of how many student trips were made. It is not a count of unique students but a count of boarding instances across all days in the period.

Total Revenue is the amount of money the school collected from student transport fees during the selected date range. This data comes from the student fee payment records. It includes all fee payments that were allocated to transport, whether paid in full or in instalments. This number represents the income side of the transport department ledger.

Total Costs is the sum of all fuel costs and all maintenance costs incurred during the selected date range. This number comes from the fuel logs and maintenance records. It represents the expense side of the transport department ledger.

Net Profit or Loss is the difference between Total Revenue and Total Costs. If Total Revenue is greater than Total Costs, the number is positive and shown as a profit in green colour. If Total Revenue is less than Total Costs, the number is negative and shown as a loss in red colour with a minus sign. If the two numbers are equal, it shows zero, meaning the department broke even. This is the single most important number on the report. The principal looks at this number first before anything else. A consistent profit across multiple months means the transport department is self-sustaining and possibly generating surplus that can be used for vehicle upgrades or driver bonuses. A consistent loss means the school is subsidising transport from other funds, which is not sustainable in the long run. The principal uses this number to decide whether transport fees need to be revised at the start of the next academic year. The report also shows this number as a percentage of revenue, so the principal can see the profit margin at a glance. For example, if Total Revenue is 1,00,000 rupees and Net Profit is 20,000 rupees, the profit margin is 20 percent, which is considered healthy.

## Charts and Visualizations

The Management Summary Report contains two charts that help the school leadership visualise financial trends over time.

The first chart is a line chart titled Revenue vs Cost Trend. The horizontal axis shows months in chronological order from left to right. If Mrs. Desai selects a date range of one month, the chart shows a single point for that month. If she selects a full year, the chart shows 12 points connected by a continuous line. There are two lines on this chart: one green line for revenue and one red line for costs. Each line connects the monthly totals to show how they change over time. Mrs. Desai can hover her mouse over any point on either line to see the exact amount for that month in a small pop-up box. This chart is powerful because it shows the relationship between income and expenses over time in a single glance. If the green line is above the red line across all months, the department is consistently profitable. If the red line crosses above the green line in any month, that month was a loss. The principal can see at a glance whether costs are growing faster than revenue. For example, if the red line is climbing steeply month after month while the green line stays flat, the principal knows that action is needed to control expenses or increase revenue. The chart also has a shaded area between the two lines to make the gap more visible. A wide gap between the lines means high profitability, while a narrow gap means the department is barely breaking even. The chart automatically adjusts its vertical axis scale based on the data, so even small changes are visible.

The second chart is a vertical bar chart titled Monthly Profit or Loss. The horizontal axis shows months in the same order as the line chart. Each month has one bar. If the net result for that month was a profit, the bar is green and extends upward from the zero line. If the net result was a loss, the bar is red and extends downward from the zero line. The height or depth of the bar shows the magnitude of the profit or loss in currency units. A reference line at zero helps the eye distinguish profit from loss immediately. Mrs. Desai can hover over any bar to see the exact profit or loss amount in a tooltip. The chart also displays the profit or loss amount directly above or below each bar so the principal can read the numbers without hovering. This chart makes it immediately obvious which months were financially good and which months were problematic. For example, if there is a very tall red bar in June, that might be the month when the annual vehicle insurance was renewed, causing a spike in costs. The principal can ask Mrs. Desai about that specific month to understand what happened. If there is a consistent pattern of red bars during certain months, the principal knows that those months have structural cost issues that need to be addressed. Both charts are connected to the date range filter and update together when the filter is changed. The charts also have a print-friendly mode that removes the hover effects and darkens the colours for better visibility on printed paper.

## Data Sources (tables used)

The Management Summary Report brings together data from five different tables in the school transport system. This is the most data-integrated report in the transport module.

The tpt_route table contains the list of all routes that the school operates. Each route record includes the route name, the start point, the end point, and the vehicle assigned. This table is used to get the Total Routes count. A route is considered active if it has at least one student assigned during the selected date range. Routes that exist in the system but had no students assigned during the period are not counted. This ensures the Total Routes number reflects actual operations rather than just the number of routes defined in the system.

The tpt_student_boarding_log table records every instance of a student boarding or unboarding a vehicle. Each record includes the student identifier, the route, the date, the boarding time, and the unboarding time if completed. This table is used to calculate the Total Boardings KPI. The system counts each boarding event as one record. If a student boards in the morning and boards again in the afternoon for the return trip, that counts as two boarding events. This gives an accurate measure of total student trips rather than just unique student counts.

The std_student_pay_log table, also called StudentPayLog, stores all fee payments made by students. Each record includes the student identifier, the amount paid, the fee type, and the date of payment. The system filters this table to include only payments that are tagged as transport fees. This table provides the Total Revenue KPI. The system sums the amount field from all matching records. If a student paid their annual transport fee of 12,000 rupees in April, that entire amount is counted in April revenue. The system does not spread the fee across multiple months. This means revenue may be higher in months when annual fees are collected and lower in other months. Mrs. Desai and the principal understand this pattern and do not interpret a low-revenue month as a problem.

The tpt_vehicle_fuel table holds records of every fuel fill for every vehicle. Each record includes the vehicle identifier, date, quantity, and amount. This table contributes to the fuel portion of Total Costs.

The tpt_vehicle_maintenance table holds records of all maintenance work done on vehicles. Each record includes the vehicle identifier, date, description, and cost. This table contributes to the maintenance portion of Total Costs.

The system sums the fuel and maintenance costs and then combines them to produce the Total Costs KPI. It then subtracts Total Costs from Total Revenue to calculate the Net Profit or Loss.

## Permissions

Access to the Management Summary Report is controlled by the permission key tenant.management-dashboard.viewAny. This is a high-level permission because the report contains sensitive financial information about the school transport operations. Only senior staff members at Green Valley School are granted this permission. Mrs. Desai has this permission because she is the Transport Coordinator and needs to prepare the report for management. The school principal and vice-principal have this permission so they can review transport finances. The accountant has this permission for audit and verification purposes. The system administrator manages these permissions through the user roles section. When a new staff member joins in any of these roles, the system administrator assigns the appropriate role, and the permissions are automatically granted. When a staff member leaves the school, their account is deactivated and the permissions are revoked automatically. Other staff members such as drivers, teachers, and administrative assistants cannot access this report by default. If Mrs. Desai needs to share the report with someone who does not have access, she exports it as a PDF document and shares it through email or in a printed meeting packet. The PDF export does not require any permission because it is generated by Mrs. Desai who already has access. However, the PDF is not password-protected, so the recipient is responsible for keeping it confidential. The permission system ensures that financial data remains confidential and is only viewed by authorised personnel.

## Who Can Access

The following roles at Green Valley School can access the Management Summary Report:

Transport Coordinator (Mrs. Desai) has full access. She is the primary user who generates and presents this report.

School Principal has view-only access. The principal relies on this report for strategic decision-making and monthly reviews.

Vice Principal has view-only access. The vice principal uses this report when the principal is unavailable or during management committee meetings.

Accountant has view-only access. The accountant uses this report to reconcile transport department finances with the school general ledger.

System Administrator has full access and can configure who else can view the report.

Drivers, teachers, parents, students, and other school staff cannot access this report.

## Example Scenario

It is the first week of July 2026 at Green Valley School. The principal has asked Mrs. Desai to present the transport department financial performance for the first term, which ran from April to June. Mrs. Desai opens the Management Summary Report. The default view shows June, the current month. She changes the date range to April 1 to June 30 and clicks Apply.

The summary bar shows Total Routes as 8, Total Boardings as 14,520, Total Revenue as 3,85,000 rupees, Total Costs as 2,92,400 rupees, and Net Profit as 92,600 rupees. The principal is pleased to see a positive number.

Mrs. Desai looks at the Revenue vs Cost Trend line chart. She sees that the green revenue line and the red cost line both trend upward from April to June. However, the green line stays above the red line in all three months, which is good. In April, revenue was 1,25,000 rupees and costs were 98,000 rupees. In May, revenue was 1,30,000 rupees and costs were 96,000 rupees. In June, revenue was 1,30,000 rupees and costs were 98,400 rupees. The lines are close but the gap is stable.

The Monthly Profit or Loss bar chart shows three green bars. April shows a profit of 27,000 rupees, May shows 34,000 rupees, and June shows 31,600 rupees. The principal notes that May was the most profitable month.

Mrs. Desai looks at the Revenue vs Cost Trend line chart more carefully. She sees that the revenue line is relatively flat across all three months, while the cost line dips slightly in May and rises again in June. She recalls that in May there was a week-long school holiday, so fewer trips were made, which explains the lower fuel cost. The cost increase in June is due to the annual vehicle insurance renewal that happens every June. She makes a note to explain these variations to the principal.

Mrs. Desai explains that the profit is healthy because the term had full student attendance and no major vehicle breakdowns. She mentions that the largest cost was fuel at 1,92,000 rupees, which is about 66 percent of total costs. The maintenance cost was 1,00,400 rupees, which is about 34 percent. She notes that this ratio is normal for a fleet of this age. The principal appreciates the clear picture and asks whether the department could save money by switching to CNG vehicles. Mrs. Desai says she will research the cost comparison and present it in the next meeting. The principal approves the continuation of the current transport fee structure for the next term.

Mrs. Desai exports the report as a PDF and also exports the underlying data as a spreadsheet for her records. She files the PDF in the transport department records folder and sends a copy to the accountant for reconciliation with the general ledger. She then schedules a follow-up meeting with the principal for the next month to review the first month of the new term.

## Logic Flow

The following describes how the Management Summary Report gathers and displays its data step by step, using plain language without any technical code terms.

Step 1: Mrs. Desai navigates to the report page. The system checks whether her user account has the tenant.management-dashboard.viewAny permission. If she has it, the page opens. If she does not have it, a message says Access Denied and advises her to contact the system administrator.

Step 2: The system sets the default date range to the current month, from the first day to the last day. For example, if today is 21 July 2026, the default range is 1 July 2026 to 31 July 2026. There are no other mandatory filters. The default date range is displayed in the filter panel at the top of the page in a clear format showing the start and end dates.

Step 3: Mrs. Desai may change the date range using the calendar picker. She selects a start date and an end date. She clicks Apply.

Step 4: The system reads the tpt_route table. It counts how many routes exist in the system. This count becomes the Total Routes KPI. The system does not filter routes by date because routes are permanent records that do not change month to month.

Step 5: The system reads the tpt_student_boarding_log table. It counts all boarding records that fall within the selected date range. Each record in the table represents one student boarding event. This count becomes the Total Boardings KPI.

Step 6: The system reads the std_student_pay_log table. It finds all payment records that have a fee type of transport and whose payment date falls within the selected date range. It adds up the amount from each matching record. The total is the Total Revenue KPI.

Step 7: The system reads the tpt_vehicle_fuel table. It finds all fuel records with dates in the selected range. It adds up the amount paid for each record. This gives the total fuel cost.

Step 8: The system reads the tpt_vehicle_maintenance table. It finds all maintenance records with dates in the selected range. It adds up the cost of each record. This gives the total maintenance cost.

Step 9: The system adds the total fuel cost and the total maintenance cost together to produce the Total Costs KPI.

Step 10: The system subtracts Total Costs from Total Revenue. If the result is positive, it is labelled Net Profit. If the result is negative, it is labelled Net Loss. If the result is zero, it is labelled Break Even.

Step 11: The system draws the line chart. For each month in the selected date range, it plots a point for total revenue and a point for total costs. It connects the revenue points with a green line and the cost points with a red line.

Step 12: The system draws the bar chart. For each month in the selected date range, it plots a bar. If the net result is positive, the bar is green and above the line. If negative, the bar is red and below the line.

Step 13: The system also calculates the monthly breakdown for the charts. For each distinct month within the selected date range, the system performs steps 5 through 10 separately. This means it calculates revenue, costs, and net profit for each individual month. These monthly values are then used to plot the line chart and the bar chart.

Step 14: The complete report is shown on the screen. The summary bar at the top shows the five KPIs for the entire selected date range. The line chart shows the month-by-month trend. The bar chart shows the month-by-month profit or loss. Below the charts, a small summary table lists each month with its revenue, costs, and net profit in a simple row format. Mrs. Desai can refer to this table if she needs exact numbers for specific months.

Step 15: If Mrs. Desai changes the date range and clicks Apply, the system repeats steps 4 through 14 with the new dates. The charts and numbers update to reflect the new period.

Step 16: Mrs. Desai can export the report in two formats. If she exports as a PDF, the system generates a document with the report title, the selected date range, the summary bar numbers, the two charts, and the monthly summary table. The PDF is formatted to print neatly on A4 paper with proper margins and page breaks. If she exports as a spreadsheet, the system creates an Excel file with two sheets. The first sheet contains the same summary view as the screen. The second sheet contains the raw monthly data with columns for month, revenue, fuel cost, maintenance cost, total cost, and net profit. This allows the principal or accountant to do further analysis if needed.

Step 17: The report includes a comparison feature. When Mrs. Desai selects a date range, the system automatically calculates the percentage change from the previous period of the same length. For example, if she selects April to June 2026, the system also calculates the totals for January to March 2026 and shows the percentage change for each KPI. This helps the principal see whether performance is improving or declining compared to the previous term. The percentage change is displayed next to each KPI in the summary bar as a small green arrow for improvement or a red arrow for decline.

Step 18: When Mrs. Desai closes the report, her session ends. The next time she opens it, the default current month view is loaded. The comparison data is cleared and recalculated fresh based on the new selection.

Step 19: The report also has a notes section at the bottom. Mrs. Desai can type comments or observations in a text box and save them as part of the report. These notes are stored in the system and are visible every time the report is opened. The notes help Mrs. Desai keep a running log of important observations, such as why a particular month had high costs or what actions were taken based on the report. When the report is exported as a PDF, the notes are included at the end of the document.

Step 20: The report supports a year-over-year comparison mode. Mrs. Desai can enable this mode by checking a box labelled Compare with Previous Year. When enabled, the system shows two sets of lines on the line chart: one for the selected date range and one in a lighter colour for the same date range in the previous year. The summary bar shows the current year values alongside the previous year values with percentage change. This is especially useful during the annual budget meeting when the management committee wants to see how this year performance compares with last year.
