# Device Setup (Attendance Device Registry) — Business Requirements

## What This Screen Does

This screen registers the mobile phones and tablets that drivers and helpers use to scan student ID cards for attendance. Each device is linked to a specific staff member — the driver or helper who carries it — and records what type of device it is (mobile or tablet), which operating system it runs, which version of the school's attendance app is installed, and the unique hardware identifier that the app uses when talking to the server. A "last seen" timestamp is updated every time the device communicates with the server, so the Transport Manager can see at a glance which devices are actively working and which ones have gone silent.

Without this screen, the system would have no way to know which devices are authorised to scan student attendance codes, whether a particular tablet is actively being used, or whether a driver's phone has the latest version of the school's attendance app installed.

The screen appears in two places:
1. **Transport Master → Device Setup tab** — Shows a list of all registered devices with a search box and filters.
2. **Dedicated Device Setup page** — Accessed directly from the main menu with the full set of options.

---

## Default Data Load

When the Transport Manager opens the Transport Master screen and clicks the Device Setup tab, the system immediately fetches all registered devices and displays them in a list, showing the most recently added ones first. The search box at the top lets the manager type a device name or hardware identifier to find a specific phone or tablet. A status filter lets them choose between viewing only devices that are currently in use or all devices including retired ones. The system also loads the list of all transport staff members — drivers and helpers — so that when the manager registers a new device, they can pick the right person from a dropdown list.

## When This Screen Is Used

- **A New Driver Joins the School and Needs a Device** — Ramesh has just been hired to drive Bus 7 for the primary school route. He does not have a school-issued phone yet. The Transport Manager must register a new tablet in the system, link it to Ramesh's name, and hand it over before his first day. Without this registration, Ramesh's tablet will not be recognised by the attendance system, and he will not be able to scan students when they board the bus.

- **A Driver's Phone Is Lost or Stolen** — On Monday morning, Sunita, the helper on Bus 3, reports that her phone was stolen from her bag during the weekend. The Transport Manager immediately opens the device record, marks it as inactive so it cannot be used for attendance scans, and notes the incident. A replacement phone is issued on Tuesday, and the new device is registered with Sunita's name. The old device's record stays in the system for audit purposes — the school has a clear log of when the theft happened and when the replacement was activated.

- **A Driver Gets a New Phone Upgrade** — The school decides to upgrade all drivers from basic Android phones to Samsung Galaxy tablets for better scanning. Prakash, who drives Bus 5, receives a new tablet. The Transport Manager updates Prakash's device record: changes the device model, updates the operating system version, and installs the latest attendance app. The old phone's record is retired so it does not appear in active lists.

- **The IT Team Needs to Check Which Devices Need an App Update** — The school's IT department releases version 3.0 of the attendance app with a new boarding pass feature. The Transport Manager opens Device Setup, sorts the list by app version, and sees that five drivers still have version 2.4.1 installed. She sends a notice to those five drivers to update their app from the school's app store. A week later, she checks again and confirms all devices are now on version 3.0.

## Key Fields at a Glance

**Device Identity and Ownership**
Every device is identified by a Universally Unique Identifier (UUID v4) that is either auto-generated by the system when the device is registered or provided by the device itself during first-time setup. This UUID is the device's permanent fingerprint — it cannot be changed and must be unique even across deleted or trashed records. A human-readable device name like "Bus 1 - Driver Phone" helps staff identify the device in lists and reports, while the device model field (e.g., "iPhone 15 Pro" or "Samsung Galaxy Tab S9") records the hardware specification.

**Staff Linkage**
Each device must be linked to a specific staff member — the driver or helper who carries it. This staff link ensures that attendance scans, push notifications, and device audit trails are all associated with the correct person. When a driver changes vehicles or leaves the school, the device assignment can be updated to a different staff member.

**Software Identification**
Four fields capture the device's software configuration: the device type (Mobile, Tablet, Laptop, or Desktop — selected from a configurable dropdown), the operating system (Android, iOS, Windows, Linux, or Mac — also dropdown-driven), the specific OS version running on the device (e.g., "14.5" for iOS or "13" for Android), and the school's attendance app version installed (e.g., "2.5.1"). This information helps the IT team identify outdated devices that need app updates.

**Communication and Activity Tracking**
The device stores a notification token that the server uses to send push notifications — for example, to alert the driver that a new route assignment has been published. The "last seen" timestamp is the most important operational field: it updates automatically every time the device communicates with the server, allowing the Transport Manager to see at a glance which devices are actively connecting and which ones have gone silent (possibly broken, lost, or stolen). The "first registered" timestamp records when the device was first added to the system.

## Business Rules

**Every Device Must Have a Unique Hardware Identifier — Even Deleted Ones**
The hardware identifier (UUID) is the single most important piece of information about a device — it is what the attendance app sends to the server with every single student scan, and the server uses this identifier to look up which device (and therefore which staff member) made the scan. If two devices, past or present, somehow shared the same identifier, the system could route notifications to the wrong phone or attribute a student's attendance to the wrong driver. Therefore, when a new device is registered, the system checks that its identifier is unique not only among currently active devices but also among devices that have been deleted from the system. This prevents anyone from reusing an old device's identifier by mistake.

**Device "Heartbeat" — The System Knows Which Devices Are Alive**
The attendance app on the driver's phone sends a quick "I am still here" signal to the server every few minutes. This signal updates the device's "last seen" timestamp. If a device stops sending this signal — because it is broken, lost, stolen, or simply turned off — the "last seen" timestamp stops updating, and the Transport Manager can see that the device has gone silent. If a device sends this signal but its hardware identifier does not match any registered device, the server rejects it — the device is unrecognised and will not be allowed to send attendance scans until it has been properly registered.

**Device Type and Operating System Are Picked from a Pre-Defined List**
The device type (Mobile, Tablet, Laptop, or Desktop) and the operating system (Android, iOS, Windows, Linux, or Mac) are selected from a dropdown menu that the school can customise through the Global Settings screen. This means the school is not stuck with a fixed list — they can add a new device type if needed. However, in practice, the standard options cover nearly every device the school will ever use.

## Workflow Steps

**Registering a New Tablet for a Driver — Mrs. Desai's Morning Task**
Mrs. Desai, the Transport Manager at Green Valley School, receives a call from the workshop that a new driver has been hired for Bus 11. She opens the Device Setup screen and clicks "Add Device." The system presents a blank registration form. She selects the new driver's name from the staff list (the system has already loaded all drivers and helpers into the dropdown), types a descriptive name like "Bus 11 - Driver Phone" so everyone can identify it, enters the device model (a Samsung Galaxy Tab A9), picks "Tablet" as the device type, "Android" as the operating system, and types the app version that IT has just installed. She leaves the hardware identifier field blank — the system automatically generates a unique one when she saves. She clicks Save. The system confirms with a green message "Device registered successfully." The new device now appears in the active devices list, linked to the new driver.

**Investigating a Silent Device — Has Bus 4's Tablet Stopped Working?**
Mrs. Desai notices that the "Last Seen" column for Bus 4's tablet shows "3 days ago" — all other devices show "a few minutes ago" or "today." She clicks on the device record to investigate. The tablet belongs to Kumar, the driver of Bus 4. She calls Kumar on his personal phone; Kumar confirms the school tablet's battery is dead and he forgot to charge it overnight. She asks him to charge it and confirm the app is running. An hour later, the "Last Seen" column updates to "a few minutes ago." The issue was simple, but without this screen, Mrs. Desai would never have known that a device had gone silent until a parent complained that their child's attendance was not being recorded.

**Retiring a Device When a Driver Leaves the School**
Rajesh, who drove Bus 7 for three years, has resigned. He returns the school-issued phone. Mrs. Desai opens the device record, clicks the status toggle to mark it as inactive, and the phone immediately disappears from all active lists. The record remains in the system with a note of when it was deactivated. Three months later, when a new driver joins, Mrs. Desai can either wipe the old record and register a fresh one or simply reactivate the same phone if it is still in working condition.

## Example Scenario — A New Driver Joins Green Valley School

Green Valley School runs 12 buses across three routes covering Whitefield, Marathahalli, and Electronic City. In June, just before the new academic year starts, the school hires a new driver named Venkatesh to replace a driver who retired. Venkatesh will drive Bus 8 on the Electronic City route, carrying 35 students to and from school every day.

Mrs. Desai, the Transport Manager, needs to set Venkatesh up with a device before his first day. She opens the Transport Master, clicks the Device Setup tab, and presses "Add Device." A blank registration form appears. She selects "Venkatesh — Driver" from the staff dropdown (the list shows every driver and helper currently employed by the school). She types "Bus 8 - Driver Phone" as the device name — a name that anyone in the transport office can instantly recognise. She enters "Samsung Galaxy M34" as the device model, picks "Mobile" as the device type, and "Android 14" as the operating system. She notes that the IT team has installed attendance app version 2.5.1 on the phone, and types that in.

The system shows a field for the hardware identifier. Mrs. Desai does not know what this is — but the system says "Leave blank to auto-generate." She leaves it blank.

She clicks Save. The system accepts the record, automatically generates a hardware identifier (a long string of letters and numbers that uniquely identifies Venkatesh's phone forever), links Venkatesh's name to the device, and displays a green success message: "Device registered successfully."

Mrs. Desai hands the phone to Venkatesh and explains how to log into the attendance app. When Venkatesh opens the app for the first time on Monday morning, the phone sends its "I am alive" signal to the server. The "Last Seen" column in Mrs. Desai's Device Setup screen now shows "Just now" for Bus 8's device. Venkatesh is ready to start scanning students.

## Requirements

- Every action on this screen — creating a device, editing it, deleting it, restoring it, or changing its status — is protected by permission checks. Only staff members with the appropriate role can perform these actions.
- All changes are recorded in the system's activity log: who made the change, what they changed, and when it happened.
- The hardware identifier can be auto-generated by the system or manually entered during registration.

---

## Who Can Access

- **Transport Manager** — Has full control over all device records. They can register new devices, edit existing ones, change which staff member a device is assigned to, mark devices as active or inactive, delete devices that are no longer needed, restore accidentally deleted devices, and permanently remove old test records. This is the primary person who works with this screen daily.

- **IT Administrator** — Can view all device details and edit software-related fields like the operating system version and app version, but cannot delete or reassign devices. This role is typically held by the school's IT support staff who manage app updates and troubleshoot device issues.

- **School Administrator** — Can view the device list and see which devices are active or inactive, which staff member each device is assigned to, and when each device last connected. They can see all information but cannot make any changes.

- **Driver or Helper** — Does not have access to this screen. Drivers and helpers receive their devices from the Transport Manager and use them only through the attendance app — they do not register or manage devices themselves.

Behind the scenes, every action on this screen is protected by a permission check in the system. If a user tries to do something they are not authorised for, the system displays an "Access Denied" message and refuses to proceed.

## Logic Flow

When the Transport Manager opens Transport Master and clicks the Device Setup tab, the system fetches all registered devices from the database and displays them in a table, with the most recently added devices shown first. The manager can type a device name or hardware identifier into the search box to find a specific phone or tablet, or use the status filter to see only active devices or only inactive ones. Each search or filter change updates the list automatically.

When the manager clicks "Add Device," the system prepares a blank registration form. It loads the list of all drivers and helpers from the staff records so the manager can select who will use this device. The manager fills in the device details — a human-readable name like "Bus 3 - Helper Tablet," the model of the phone or tablet, the type of device (mobile or tablet), the operating system it runs, and the version of the attendance app installed. The hardware identifier field can be left blank — if the manager does not enter one, the system will generate a unique identifier automatically when the form is saved.

Before saving, the system checks that the hardware identifier (if manually entered) does not match any existing identifier — not even one that belongs to a deleted device. If a conflict is found, the form displays an error message and refuses to save. The system also checks that a staff member has been selected, that the device name is not empty, and that the device type and operating system are valid options from the pre-configured lists. If anything is wrong, the problematic fields are highlighted and the manager must correct them before trying again.

If everything is correct, the system creates the device record, links it to the selected staff member, generates a hardware identifier if one was not provided, records the action in the activity log with the manager's name and the current time, and returns to the device list with a green success message. The new device now appears in the list and is ready to be used by the driver or helper.

When the manager clicks Edit on an existing device, the form loads with all the current values pre-filled. The manager can update the device name, change which staff member is assigned to it, update the OS version or app version (for example, after an app update), or change the active status. The system records the change in the activity log. If the manager marks the device as inactive, it immediately disappears from active lists and will not be recognised for attendance scans until it is toggled back on.

When the manager deletes a device, the system does not erase it permanently. Instead, it hides the device from the main list and moves it to the "Trash" folder. The record continues to exist, and all historical attendance data linked to that device remains intact. The manager can view trashed devices by switching to the Trash view, where they have the option to restore a device (which brings it back to the active list) or permanently delete it (which removes it from the database entirely — typically done only for test entries).

For the "last seen" mechanism: every registered device has an attendance app installed that sends a short "I am alive" signal to the server every few minutes. When the server receives this signal, it updates the "last seen" timestamp for that device. If the server receives a signal from a device whose hardware identifier does not match any registered device, it silently ignores the signal — the device is unrecognised and will not be allowed to submit attendance scans until it has been properly registered. This is how the system ensures that only authorised devices can record student attendance.

## Validate Before Save — What the System Checks Before Registering a Device

| Field in the Form | What the System Checks | Error Message If Wrong |
|-------------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Hardware Identifier | Optional — if left blank, a unique identifier is created automatically. If entered manually, it must be a valid format and must not match any existing or deleted device. | "A device with this hardware identifier is already registered. Please check and try again." |
| Device Name | Must be provided, up to 100 characters. A descriptive name like "Bus 3 - Driver Phone" helps staff identify the device. | "Please enter a device name." |
| Staff Member | Must be selected from the list of drivers and helpers. Each device must be linked to a specific person. | "Please select a staff member for this device." |
| Device Type | Must be selected from the pre-configured options (Mobile, Tablet, Laptop, or Desktop). | "Please select a device type." |
| Operating System | Must be selected from the pre-configured options (Android, iOS, Windows, Linux, or Mac). | "Please select an operating system." |

## Error Handling — What Can Go Wrong

| Problem | What the User Sees | What Type of Issue |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Hardware identifier already exists (entered manually) | "A device with this hardware identifier is already registered. Please check and try again." — The form does not submit until a unique identifier is entered. | Data entry error — prevented before saving |
| No staff member selected | "Please select a staff member for this device." — The form does not submit. | Data entry error — prevented before saving |
| Device name left blank | "Please enter a device name." — The form does not submit. | Data entry error — prevented before saving |
| User tries to register a device without authorisation | The system displays an "Access Denied" message and blocks the action entirely. | Permission error — system blocks the action |
| Device sends a heartbeat signal but its hardware identifier is not recognised | The server silently ignores the signal. The device's "Last Seen" timestamp never updates, and the device cannot submit attendance scans. The driver sees an error on the app like "Device not registered. Please contact Transport Manager." | Operational error — requires registration |
| Device stops sending heartbeat signals (broken, lost, or battery dead) | The "Last Seen" timestamp stops updating. The Transport Manager sees "3 days ago" next to the device in the list. The device remains active in the system — no automatic deactivation occurs. The manager must manually mark it as inactive. | 🔴 Gap — No automatic flagging of silent devices |
| Device deleted while still being used by a driver | The deletion succeeds. The device disappears from the active list. However, if the driver continues using the attendance app on that phone, the server rejects its scans because the hardware identifier no longer matches an active device. The driver's app shows an error the next time they try to scan a student. | 🔴 Gap — No warning before deleting a recently active device |

## Failure Scenarios — What Could Go Wrong

**FC-001 — Device Hardware Identifier Conflict After Restoring a Deleted Record**
Mrs. Desai deletes a test device that was registered with a manually entered hardware identifier. Six months later, a new staff member joins and the IT team registers a new device, manually entering a hardware identifier that — by coincidence — matches the deleted test device. The system rejects the registration because the identifier already exists in the trash. Mrs. Desai must permanently delete the old test record before the new device can be registered. This causes a 10-minute delay while she investigates why the registration is failing.

**FC-002 — A Device Goes Silent but the System Takes No Action**
A driver's phone falls into a water puddle during the monsoon season and stops working. The driver does not report it immediately because he is new and does not know the procedure. The device's "Last Seen" timestamp shows "12 days ago" but the system continues to show it as active. No automatic alert is sent to the Transport Manager. Meanwhile, the 30 students on that bus are not being scanned for attendance because the driver cannot open the app. Mrs. Desai only discovers the issue when a parent calls the school complaining that their child's attendance record shows "Not Marked" for the past two weeks. The system has no way to automatically flag or send a notification about devices that have stopped communicating.

**FC-003 — Device Reassigned to a Different Staff Member Without Clearing Old Data**
A tablet that was used by Driver Rajesh for two years is reassigned to a new driver, Suresh, when Rajesh resigns. Mrs. Desai updates the staff member field in the device record from Rajesh to Suresh. The system accepts the change and logs it. However, the tablet still has Rajesh's old app session and cached data. Suresh opens the attendance app and sees Rajesh's route information instead of his own. IT support must be called to wipe the tablet's app data and reconfigure it for Suresh. The system does not provide a "Reset device for new user" option or warn the manager when a device is reassigned.

**FC-004 — Transport Manager Cannot Retrieve a Device's Hardware Identifier to Configure a Replacement Phone**
An existing driver's phone is stolen. The school purchases an identical replacement phone. Mrs. Desai opens the old device record to copy the hardware identifier into the new phone's configuration, but the identifier is a long string of 36 characters with hyphens that is not easy to manually transcribe. She must contact the IT department to extract the identifier from the system. This takes two hours, during which the driver cannot scan students because the new phone is not yet recognised by the system.
