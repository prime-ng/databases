# Driver and Attendant Report

## What This Report Shows

Mrs. Desai, the Transport Supervisor at Green Valley School, uses the Driver and Attendant Report to monitor the performance of every driver and helper who works in the school transport department. This report is her primary tool for personnel management. It tells her who is doing well, who needs improvement, and who might need additional training or support.

The report goes beyond simple attendance tracking. It combines attendance records with trip execution data and incident logs to create a complete performance picture for each staff member. Mrs. Desai can see at a glance how many trips each driver or attendant handled, whether they were on time, how regular their attendance has been, and whether they have been involved in any incidents.

This report answers questions like: Which drivers have the best on-time record this month? Which attendants have the highest attendance rate? Are there staff members who are consistently involved in incidents? How does each driver's performance compare to the team average? Has a particular driver's punctuality improved or declined over the past few weeks?

Mrs. Desai values this report because it helps her make fair and data-driven decisions about assignments, training needs, and performance feedback. Instead of relying on memory or recent impressions, she can review actual numbers and trends. This has made her team management more transparent and has improved morale because staff members know that their performance is tracked objectively.

## Default Data Load / Filters

When the Driver and Attendant Report opens, the system automatically loads data for the current academic month. Mrs. Desai prefers this monthly view because it gives enough data points to identify meaningful trends while staying focused on recent performance. The default view includes all staff members across both driver and helper roles.

The report provides the following filter options:

Date Range filter lets Mrs. Desai choose any custom period for performance review. She uses this at the end of each term to generate the term-wise performance summary that goes into each staff member's file. The date range can be set to a single week for a quick check, a full month for regular monitoring, or an entire term for formal evaluations.

Role filter allows Mrs. Desai to view either drivers only, attendants or helpers only, or both roles together. This is useful because drivers and helpers have different responsibilities and their performance metrics carry different weight. For example, a driver's on-time performance is more critical to route execution, while a helper's attendance directly affects boarding accuracy.

Staff filter lists all individual drivers and helpers alphabetically. Mrs. Desai uses this filter when she needs to review a specific person, perhaps because a parent has given feedback about that staff member or because she noticed a change in their performance during the daily trip review. She can select one person or multiple people for comparison.

All filters work together. When Mrs. Desai selects a specific role and a specific date range, the report recalculates every metric to reflect only the matching data. She can clear all filters with one click to return to the default monthly view.

## When This Report Is Used

Mrs. Desai reviews the Driver and Attendant Report every Monday morning as part of her weekly planning routine. She checks the previous week's attendance and trip handling numbers to decide the weekly staffing schedule. If a driver has been absent frequently, she may need to plan for a backup driver and have a conversation about the absences.

At the beginning of every month, Mrs. Desai generates the monthly performance snapshot. She shares this with each driver and attendant during their one-on-one meetings. These meetings are constructive in nature, where she highlights good performance and discusses areas for improvement. The report gives her concrete numbers to reference rather than vague observations.

The school's Human Resources department requests a summary of this report every quarter. They use it as input for the annual performance appraisal of transport staff. Mrs. Desai provides the attendance rate, incident count, and overall performance score for each staff member. This data directly influences decisions about increments, bonuses, and promotions.

When a new driver or attendant joins the team, Mrs. Desai uses this report to track their progress during the probation period. She closely monitors their first three months of attendance, punctuality, and incident involvement. If the new staff member shows strong performance, she confirms their permanent placement. If there are concerns, she uses the report data to structure a performance improvement plan.

The report is also used reactively when an incident occurs. If a driver is involved in a traffic incident or receives a parent complaint, Mrs. Desai pulls up their performance history from this report. She can see whether this is an isolated event or part of a pattern. This context helps her decide on the appropriate response, whether it is a warning, retraining, or more serious action.

## Key Metrics / KPIs

Staff Name displays the full name of each driver or attendant as recorded in the school personnel system. The name is clickable, and clicking it opens a detailed drill-down view that shows the individual's trip-by-trip record for the selected period. Mrs. Desai uses this to quickly navigate to a specific person's detailed performance data.

Role indicates whether the staff member is a Driver or a Helper. The school distinguishes between these two roles because their responsibilities differ significantly. Drivers are primarily responsible for vehicle operation, route navigation, and timely arrival at stops. Helpers or attendants are responsible for student boarding, safety supervision inside the vehicle, and communication with parents. The Role column allows Mrs. Desai to apply the right performance expectations to each person.

Attendance Rate is a percentage calculated by dividing the number of days the staff member was present for their scheduled duty by the total number of days they were scheduled to work. A one hundred percent attendance rate means the staff member never missed a single scheduled day. Mrs. Desai expects all transport staff to maintain at least ninety-five percent attendance. Anything below this threshold triggers a conversation about the reasons for absence. The report also highlights any patterns in absences, such as a tendency to miss Monday shifts.

Trips Handled counts the total number of trips that the staff member participated in during the selected period. For drivers, this means trips where they were behind the wheel. For helpers, this means trips where they were on board assisting with student management. Mrs. Desai uses this metric to understand each person's workload. If one driver is handling significantly more trips than others, it may indicate an imbalance in route assignments that needs correction.

Average Delay is calculated specifically for each driver based on the trips they operated. The system measures the difference between the scheduled arrival time at each stop and the actual arrival time, then averages these differences across all stops and all trips handled by that driver. A lower average delay indicates better punctuality. Mrs. Desai tracks this metric closely because punctuality is the most common source of parent complaints. Drivers who consistently show an average delay below five minutes are recognised as top performers.

Incidents Count is the total number of incidents involving the staff member during the selected period. Incidents can include traffic violations, student injuries, vehicle damage, behavioural issues with parents or students, and failure to follow safety procedures. Mrs. Desai investigates every incident regardless of count, but a high incidents count or a rising trend is a serious concern that may lead to disciplinary action or removal from duty.

Performance Score is a composite metric that Mrs. Desai uses as a quick reference for each staff member's overall performance. The system calculates this score by combining the attendance rate, average delay, trips handled, and incidents count using a weighted formula. The score ranges from zero to one hundred. A score above eighty is considered good, between sixty and eighty is average, and below sixty indicates the need for improvement. This single number helps Mrs. Desai quickly identify which staff members are performing well and which ones need attention.

## Charts and Visualizations

The Driver and Attendant Report includes two charts that help Mrs. Desai identify trends and compare staff performance visually.

The first chart is a Bar chart that shows staff-wise trips handled. Each bar represents one staff member, and the height of the bar shows how many trips they handled during the selected period. The bars are sorted in descending order, so the staff member with the highest trip count appears first. This chart helps Mrs. Desai quickly identify workload distribution across her team. If she sees a very tall bar next to several very short bars, it signals an unequal distribution that needs rebalancing. The chart is colour-coded by role, with drivers in one colour and helpers in a different colour, so Mrs. Desai can see the split at a glance.

The second chart is a Line chart that shows attendance trend over time. The horizontal axis represents time periods such as weeks or months, and the vertical axis shows the attendance rate percentage. Each staff member is represented by a separate line. Mrs. Desai uses this chart to track whether attendance is improving or declining across the team. If she sees a downward trend across multiple staff members, it may indicate a broader issue such as low morale or dissatisfaction. If only one staff member's line is dropping while others remain stable, it points to an individual issue that needs personal attention.

Both charts are interactive. Mrs. Desai can click on any bar in the bar chart to see the detailed trip list for that staff member. She can click on any line in the line chart to see the weekly attendance breakdown for that person. The charts update automatically when she changes the filters.

## Data Sources

The Driver and Attendant Report pulls data from five main tables in the transport module.

The tpt_personnel table is the master list of all transport staff. It contains each person's full name, employee code, role designation, date of joining, contact details, licence information for drivers, and employment status. The report uses this table as the starting point, listing every active driver and helper in the team.

The tpt_driver_attendance table is referenced in the system as attendance. This table records daily attendance for each transport staff member. Each record contains the staff identifier, the date, and the attendance status which can be Present, Absent, or Leave. The report uses this table to calculate the attendance rate for each staff member by counting the number of Present records divided by the total number of scheduled working days.

The tpt_trip table is referenced in two contexts. For drivers, the system looks at the driverTrips relationship which links trip records to the driver assigned for that trip. For helpers, the system looks at the helperTrips relationship which links trip records to the helper assigned for that trip. Each trip record includes the scheduled and actual times, which the report uses to calculate the average delay for each driver.

The tpt_trip_incidents table is referenced in the system as incidents. This table stores records of any incidents that occurred during a trip. Each incident record includes the trip identifier, the incident type, a description, the date and time, and the staff members involved. The report counts incident records linked to each staff member to generate the Incidents Count metric.

The tpt_trip_stop_detail table provides stop-level timing data. The report uses this table to calculate the actual arrival time at each stop for each trip handled by a driver. By comparing scheduled stop times from this table with actual stop times, the system can accurately calculate the delay per stop and then aggregate it to the driver level.

## Permissions

The Driver and Attendant Report is protected by the permission key tenant.driver-performance.viewAny. This permission controls access to all driver and attendant performance data across the transport module. When a user tries to open this report, the system checks whether their role includes this permission. If it does not, the report page displays a message indicating that access is restricted.

The permission is scoped at the tenant level. This means that in a multi-tenant setup where Green Valley School is one of several schools using the same system, a supervisor at one school cannot view the driver performance data of another school. Each school's transport staff data remains completely separate and private.

## Who Can Access

At Green Valley School, access to the Driver and Attendant Report is granted to specific roles based on their need to review staff performance.

Mrs. Desai as the Transport Supervisor has full access to this report. She is the primary user and uses it for weekly performance reviews, monthly evaluations, and quarterly reporting to HR. She can view all staff across both roles and can drill down into individual performance details.

The Transport Coordinator also has access to this report. The coordinator uses it to support Mrs. Desai in day-to-day staff management. When Mrs. Desai is on leave, the coordinator reviews the report to ensure that any performance issues are still being tracked and addressed. The coordinator cannot modify any performance data but can view and export the report.

The Human Resources Manager has access to a slightly summarised version of this report. HR does not need to see every trip-level detail but requires the attendance rate, incidents count, and performance score for each staff member. These are used during the annual appraisal cycle and for maintaining personnel records.

The School Principal has access to an executive summary of this report. The principal can see the overall team performance trends, the number of staff members in each performance category, and any significant changes compared to the previous period. The principal does not access individual staff details unless there is a specific reason such as a serious incident review.

Drivers and attendants cannot access this report. The data is intended for management and supervisory review only. However, Mrs. Desai voluntarily shares a personal performance summary with each staff member during their monthly one-on-one meeting as part of her transparent management approach.

## Example Scenario

It is the first Monday of the month, and Mrs. Desai opens the Driver and Attendant Report to review February performance. The default monthly view loads with all staff members listed. She notices that the Performance Score column shows a staff member named Rajesh Kumar with a score of fifty-two, which is well below the sixty-point threshold.

Mrs. Desai looks at Rajesh's individual metrics. His Attendance Rate is seventy-eight percent, which means he missed more than one out of every five scheduled days in February. The Trips Handled count is twenty-two, which is significantly lower than the team average of thirty-five. His Average Delay is eighteen minutes, which is the highest among all drivers. The Incidents Count shows two incidents, while most other drivers have zero.

She clicks on Rajesh's name to open the detailed view. The attendance trend line chart shows that his attendance has been declining month over month. In December, he was at ninety percent. In January, he dropped to eighty-five percent. In February, he fell to seventy-eight percent. This is a clear downward trend.

The trip-by-trip list shows that Rajesh was assigned to Route 9A - Janakpuri but several of his trips were taken over by backup drivers because he did not report for duty. On the days he did work, his arrival at stops was consistently late by fifteen to twenty minutes. The two incidents logged against him were both minor, one involving a delayed start due to late arrival at the depot and another involving a navigation error that added extra time to the route.

Mrs. Desai recalls that Rajesh had been a reliable driver for three years before this decline. She decides not to take immediate disciplinary action. Instead, she schedules a meeting with Rajesh for Tuesday afternoon. During the meeting, she shares the report data with him in a constructive manner, showing the attendance trend and the delay numbers. Rajesh explains that he has been dealing with a personal family health issue that has affected his sleep schedule and punctuality.

Mrs. Desai works with Rajesh to create a temporary arrangement. She adjusts his start time by fifteen minutes later to accommodate his situation for the next month and refers him to the Employee Assistance Programme offered by the school. She sets a review date for the end of March and saves the current report as a baseline.

At the end of March, Mrs. Desai opens the report again. Rajesh's attendance rate has recovered to ninety-two percent. His average delay is down to six minutes. There are no new incidents. His performance score has risen to seventy-five. Mrs. Desai marks the improvement in her notes and continues the adjusted schedule for another month to ensure the change is sustainable.

## Logic Flow

The Driver and Attendant Report processes data through a structured series of steps to generate accurate performance metrics for each staff member.

The first step is personnel identification. The system queries the tpt_personnel table to retrieve all active staff members whose role is either Driver or Helper. These form the base list of people who will appear in the report. Inactive or terminated staff members are excluded unless specifically requested through an advanced filter.

The second step is filter application. The system applies the selected date range, role, and staff filters to narrow down the data. The date range filter affects all subsequent calculations because it determines which attendance records, trips, and incidents are included. The role filter limits the personnel list to only the selected role category. The staff filter further narrows the list to selected individuals.

The third step is attendance calculation. For each staff member in the filtered list, the system queries the tpt_driver_attendance table to find all attendance records within the selected date range. It counts the total number of scheduled working days, which is the total days minus weekends and declared holidays. It counts the number of days marked Present. The Attendance Rate is calculated by dividing Present days by total scheduled working days and multiplying by one hundred to get a percentage.

The fourth step is trip count calculation. For drivers, the system queries the tpt_trip table using the driverTrips relationship to count all trips where this staff member is listed as the driver within the selected date range. For helpers, the system uses the helperTrips relationship instead. The count of these trips becomes the Trips Handled metric for that staff member.

The fifth step is delay calculation. For each trip handled by a staff member, the system retrieves the stop-level data from the tpt_trip_stop_detail table. It calculates the delay for each stop as the difference between the actual arrival time and the scheduled arrival time. It then averages these delays across all stops for a single trip delay, and then averages across all trips to get the staff member's average delay. This calculation applies only to drivers, as helpers do not have direct control over arrival timing.

The sixth step is incident counting. The system queries the tpt_trip_incidents table and counts all incident records where the staff member is listed as involved. The count is filtered by the selected date range. This count becomes the Incidents Count metric.

The seventh step is performance score computation. The system takes the four calculated metrics, Attendance Rate, Trips Handled, Average Delay, and Incidents Count, and applies a weighted formula. Each metric is normalised to a zero to one hundred scale. Attendance Rate is weighted at thirty percent. Trips Handled is weighted at twenty percent. Average Delay is inverted so that lower delays produce higher scores and is weighted at thirty percent. Incidents Count is also inverted so that fewer incidents produce higher scores and is weighted at twenty percent. The weighted scores are summed to produce the final Performance Score.

The eighth step is chart generation. The system creates the bar chart by grouping staff by their Trips Handled count, with one bar per staff member sorted in descending order. The system creates the line chart by grouping attendance rates by week or month for each staff member. The attendance data is plotted as separate lines to show trends over time.

The ninth step is data display. The system renders a table with one row per staff member showing all calculated metrics. The table is sortable by any column. Each staff name is a hyperlink that opens a detailed drill-down page showing their individual trip-by-trip record, attendance dates, and incident details. The charts and summary KPIs are displayed above the table for quick reference.

The report handles several special conditions in its logic. If a staff member has no trips in the selected period, their Trips Handled shows zero and their Average Delay is shown as not applicable because there are no trips to measure. Their Performance Score is calculated based only on attendance and incidents, with the trip-related weights redistributed. If a staff member has no attendance records in the selected period, perhaps because they were on long leave, the Attendance Rate shows as not applicable and the score is recalculated excluding the attendance weight.

The system also manages data privacy within the report. When Mrs. Desai exports the report as a PDF for sharing with the HR department, the export version automatically redacts certain personal details such as contact numbers and addresses that are visible in the on-screen version. This ensures that sensitive staff information is not circulated outside the transport management team. The export also includes a header with the report generation date and filter criteria so that recipients know exactly what data the report covers.

The report logic includes a trend detection feature. When the system calculates the current period's metrics, it also compares them with the previous period's data. If a staff member's Attendance Rate has dropped by more than ten percentage points, or their Average Delay has increased by more than five minutes, or their Incidents Count has doubled, the system automatically highlights these changes with a visual indicator next to the metric. This trend detection helps Mrs. Desai spot deteriorating performance without having to manually compare reports from different dates.

A caching mechanism is built into the report logic to ensure fast loading times. When Mrs. Desai first opens the report for a given date range, the system calculates all metrics and stores the results in a temporary cache. If she opens the same date range again within the same day, the system serves the cached results instead of recalculating everything. The cache is automatically cleared when new data is entered, such as when a new trip is completed or attendance is marked, ensuring that Mrs. Desai never sees stale data.

The report's logic also includes a comparative benchmarking feature. After calculating each staff member's individual metrics, the system computes team-wide averages for Attendance Rate, Trips Handled, Average Delay, and Incidents Count. Each staff member's metrics are displayed alongside the team average in the detailed table, with up and down arrows showing whether they are above or below the average. This benchmarking helps Mrs. Desai identify top performers who can serve as mentors and staff members who may need additional support without relying on subjective judgement.

A seasonality adjustment is built into the attendance calculation. The system recognises that attendance patterns differ between regular school periods and examination periods, holiday weeks, and special event days. When calculating Attendance Rate, the system excludes days that were declared as non-transport days by the school administration, such as days when only half the school attended for parent-teacher meetings or sports day events. This prevents unfairly penalising staff members for days when transport operations were not running at full capacity.

The report logic also supports a what-if analysis feature for Mrs. Desai's planning needs. She can create a hypothetical scenario by adjusting a staff member's attendance rate or delay numbers to see how their Performance Score would change. For example, she can ask the system to show what Rajesh's score would be if his attendance improved from seventy-eight percent to ninety percent. This helps her set realistic improvement targets during performance discussions and show staff members the direct relationship between their daily behaviour and their overall performance rating.
