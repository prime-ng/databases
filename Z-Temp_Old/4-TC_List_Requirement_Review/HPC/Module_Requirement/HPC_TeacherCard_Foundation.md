# HPC Teacher Card — Foundation Template (BV1)
**For grades:** Nursery, LKG, UKG, 1st, 2nd, 3rd
**Total Pages:** 18 pages (tabs)

## What is this?

This is the main screen where the class teacher fills the Holistic Progress Card for young students (Nursery to Class 3). Think of it like a big digital form that has 18 different pages or "tabs" (like tabs in a folder). The teacher clicks on each tab to fill in information about the student. All pages save together when the teacher clicks the Save button at the bottom.

The computer screen shows one page at a time. At the top of the screen, you see 18 small labels (tabs) in a row. Each tab has a name like "Page 1," "Page 2," "Page 3," and so on. You click a tab and that page opens. The tab you are on looks different (it is highlighted or has a different color) so you know which page you are working on.

## Page 1 — School & Student Information (The "Who is this student?" page)

This page shows all the basic details about the school and the student. Most fields fill themselves automatically from the school's computer system. The teacher does NOT type them. The teacher just checks if everything is correct. If something is wrong, the teacher can change it.

Think of this page like the front page of a file folder. It has the student's name, the school's name, the parents' names, and other basic information. You read it to make sure you are looking at the right student's card.

### School Details (auto-filled — you don't need to type these)

These details come from the school's information stored in the computer when the school was first set up. The principal or the school admin enters this information once, and it shows up on every student's card automatically.

- **School Name:** The full name of the school (e.g., "Delhi Public School"). This comes from the school's registration records. You cannot change this unless you are an admin.
- **Address:** The street address of the school. Comes from the school's master data.
- **Village / Town:** Which village or town the school is in. Comes from school records.
- **BRC:** Block Resource Centre — a local education office area. This is a government code.
- **CRC:** Cluster Resource Centre — a smaller education cluster. Another government code.
- **State:** Which state the school is in (e.g., Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka).
- **Pincode:** The postal code of the school — shown as 6 separate boxes, one for each digit (e.g., 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1). Each box accepts only one number. You click in the first box, type "1," then the cursor moves to the next box automatically.
- **UDISE Code:** A special 11-digit number the government gives to every school — shown as 11 separate boxes. Like the pincode, you type one digit per box. This number is like the school's government ID number.
- **Medium of Instruction:** What language the school teaches in (e.g., Hindi, English, Urdu). This is set by the school.
- **Rural / Urban:** Whether the school is in a village (rural) or a city (urban). This is set when the school registers.
- **Affiliation Number:** The school's board affiliation number (for CBSE, ICSE, etc.).
- **Email:** School's email address (e.g., "contact@delhipublicschool.edu").
- **Website:** School's website address (e.g., "www.delhipublicschool.edu").
- **Phone Numbers:** School's landline or mobile numbers.

### Student Details (auto-filled from the school records)

These details come from the admission records. When the student joined the school, the office staff entered all this information. It shows up here automatically. The teacher never types a student's name.

- **First Name, Middle Name, Last Name:** The student's full name. Comes from the admission form.
- **Admission Number:** The unique number the school gave the student when they joined. This is like the student's ID number for the school.
- **Gender:** Boy or girl. Comes from the admission form.
- **Date of Birth:** The student's birth date. Comes from the birth certificate that was submitted at admission. Shows as day/month/year (e.g., 15/03/2018).
- **Aadhar ID:** The student's 12-digit government ID number. Each digit has its own box.
- **APAAR ID:** Another government ID for education (like Aadhar but for school). Also called "Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry." Think of it as a lifelong student ID number that stays with the student even if they change schools.
- **Birth Certificate Number:** The number on the student's birth certificate.
- **Student Photo:** A space to see or upload the student's photograph. If the school took a photo during admission, it shows here. If not, there is a button to upload one. Click "Upload" and select a picture file from your computer.

### Parent / Guardian Details (auto-filled from school records)

These come from the admission form. The office typed them when the parents filled the admission papers.

- **Father's Name:** Full name of the father (e.g., "Rajesh Kumar Sharma").
- **Mother's Name:** Full name of the mother (e.g., "Sunita Sharma").
- **Guardian Name:** If someone else takes care of the child (grandparent, uncle, etc.), their name goes here. If the parents are the guardians, this can be left empty.
- **Father's Phone, Mother's Phone, Guardian's Phone:** Mobile numbers. These are 10-digit numbers.
- **Father's Occupation, Mother's Occupation, Guardian's Occupation:** What they do for work (e.g., "Teacher," "Farmer," "Business," "Doctor").
- **Father's Email, Mother's Email, Guardian's Email:** Email addresses.

### Contact Information (auto-filled)

- **Mobile:** The family's mobile number.
- **Email:** Family email address.
- **Address:** Home address of the student (e.g., "House 42, Street 5, Green Park Colony").
- **Pincode:** Home pincode (again shown as 6 boxes).

### Academic Information (auto-filled)

- **Roll Number:** The student's roll number in the class (e.g., "12").
- **Class:** Which class/grade (e.g., Nursery, UKG, 1st). The teacher selects the class when opening the card.
- **Section:** Which section (A, B, C, etc.). Comes from the class assignment.
- **House:** Which school house the student belongs to (e.g., Red House, Blue House, Green House, Yellow House). Think of houses like teams in school.

### Physical Measurements (auto-filled from health records)

- **Height:** In centimeters (e.g., "110 cm").
- **Weight:** In kilograms (e.g., "18 kg").
These come from the school health checkup records. If the school nurse has entered the measurements, they appear here.

### Demographics (auto-filled)

- **Religion:** The student's religion (e.g., "Hindu," "Muslim," "Sikh," "Christian").
- **Caste:** The student's caste category (e.g., "General," "SC," "ST," "OBC").
- **Nationality:** The student's nationality (e.g., "Indian").
- **Mother Tongue:** The language spoken at home (e.g., "Hindi," "Bengali," "Tamil").
- **Blood Group:** The student's blood type (A+, B-, O+, etc.).
- **EWS Status:** Whether the student belongs to the Economically Weaker Section (Yes or No). This is a government category for students from low-income families.

### Attendance Table — Full Example with Real Numbers

This is a table that looks like a grid. It shows attendance for each month of the school year, from April to March. Think of it like a calendar that counts how many days the student came to school.

**How the table looks on screen:**

You see a grid with columns going across the top and rows going down. The columns are:
| Month | Total Working Days | Days Present | Attendance % | Reason for Absence |

Each row is one month. The first row is April, the second is May, the third is June, and so on until March.

**Example of a filled table for a student named Ravi:**

| Month | Total Working Days | Days Present | Attendance % | Reason for Absence |
|-------|-------------------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| April | 22 | 20 | 90.9% | Was sick with fever for 2 days |
| May | 20 | 19 | 95.0% | |
| June | 18 | 15 | 83.3% | Family went on a trip for 3 days |
| July | 23 | 22 | 95.7% | |
| August | 22 | 22 | 100% | |
| September | 21 | 18 | 85.7% | Had chickenpox for 5 days |
| October | 18 | 17 | 94.4% | |
| November | 20 | 20 | 100% | |
| December | 19 | 18 | 94.7% | |
| January | 22 | 21 | 95.5% | |
| February | 20 | 20 | 100% | |
| March | 21 | 20 | 95.2% | |

**Row-by-row explanation:**

- **Row 1 (April):** School was open 22 days in April. Ravi came for 20 days. So his attendance is 20 divided by 22, which equals 90.9%. The teacher wrote "Was sick with fever for 2 days" in the Reason column. If the student was absent a lot, the teacher explains why here.
- **Row 2 (May):** School was open 20 days. Ravi came 19 days. 95% attendance. No reason needed because he was only absent 1 day.
- **Row 3 (June):** School was open 18 days. Ravi came 15 days. 83.3%. The teacher wrote "Family went on a trip for 3 days." This is lower attendance, so a reason is helpful.
- **Row 7 (October):** 18 working days, 17 present. The Reason cell is empty because 1 day absence does not need explanation.

**What each column means:**

- **Month:** Which month of the school year. Starts with April (first month of the school year) and ends with March.
- **Total Working Days:** How many days school was open that month. This number does not include Sundays and holidays. For example, in April there might be 22 school days if there are 30 days in the month minus 8 Sundays/holidays.
- **Days Present:** How many days the student actually came to school. The teacher types this number. For example, if the student was absent 2 days in a month with 22 days, the teacher types "20."
- **Attendance Percentage:** The computer calculates this automatically. You do NOT type this. The formula is: Days Present divided by Total Working Days, multiplied by 100. For 20 out of 22: 20 ÷ 22 × 100 = 90.9%. The number is shown with one decimal place (one digit after the dot).
- **Reason for Absence:** If the student was absent a lot (more than 2-3 days in a month), the teacher can type a note explaining why. Examples: "Was sick with fever," "Family went on a trip," "Had chickenpox." For short absences (1 day), you can leave this blank.

**How to add or remove rows:**

- **Adding a row:** Look at the bottom of the table. There is a button with a **+** (plus) sign on it. Click this button. A new empty row appears at the bottom. You can then type the month name and the numbers. Why would you add a row? If the school year has a special term or extra month, you can add it. For example, if the student joined late and you need an extra row for their partial month.
- **Removing a row:** Each row has a button with a **-** (minus) sign on the right side. Click this button. A small popup window appears that asks "Are you sure you want to delete this row?" Click "Yes" to remove the row. Click "No" or "Cancel" to keep it. The row disappears only if you click Yes.
- **Adding a column:** In some versions, there is also a button to add new columns. For example, if the school wants a column called "Remarks" or "Verified By," they can add it. This is usually done by the school admin, not the teacher.

## Page 2 — Me and My Surroundings (Child-Friendly Profile Page)

This is a colorful, fun page designed for young students. Think of it like an "All About Me" poster that students make in class. The teacher sits with the student and helps them fill it. It uses emojis, pictures, and simple dropdown menus so it is easy to understand. The student can click on things themselves with the teacher's help.

### This is Me

- **Gender Selection:** You see two emojis on the screen — one is a girl emoji (👧) and one is a boy emoji (👦). They are big and colorful. **How it works:** When the student clicks the girl emoji, it gets a pink border around it. When they click the boy emoji, it gets a blue border around it. The selected emoji stays highlighted. The student picks the one that matches their gender. Only one can be selected at a time. If you click the girl emoji and then click the boy emoji, the girl emoji's border goes away and the boy emoji gets highlighted. The selected value is saved automatically — the teacher does not need to click anything else.
- **Age:** A dropdown list (like a menu you pull down). It has a small arrow on the right side. Click the arrow. A list of numbers appears: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8. Click on the student's age. The list closes and the selected age shows in the box. Example: If the student is 5 years old, click "5."
- **Birthday:** A date picker. You see a box that shows "dd/mm/yyyy" or is empty. Click the box. A small calendar pops up on the screen. The calendar shows the current month. You can click the left or right arrows to go to previous or next months. Click on the student's birth date (e.g., 15th). The calendar closes and the date appears in the box as "15/03/2019."
- **City:** A text box where the teacher types the name of the city the student lives in. Example: "Delhi," "Mumbai," "Lucknow." The teacher clicks in the box and types the city name.

### My Family

- **Family Member Toggles:** These are clickable emoji buttons. Each one has a picture. They are arranged in a row:
  - 👧 (Girl — this can mean a sister or the student themselves)
  - 👦 (Boy — this can mean a brother)
  - 👨 (Man — this means father or grandfather)
  - 👩 (Woman — this means mother or grandmother)

  **How they work:** Click on an emoji once — it turns blue or gets a colored border to show it is "selected" or "turned on." Click it again — it goes back to normal to show it is "deselected" or "turned off." Think of it like pressing a light switch. Press once = light on. Press again = light off. Multiple emojis can be selected at the same time.

  Examples:
  - If the student lives with mother and father, they click the woman emoji AND the man emoji. Both stay highlighted.
  - If the student lives with mother, father, and an older sister, they click the woman, man, AND girl emojis.
  - If the student lives with only their grandmother, they click the woman emoji only.
  - If the student lives with their uncle, they click the man emoji.
  
  The teacher helps the student understand which emoji to click. This helps the student feel comfortable and share about their family.

### My Future

- **Dream Job:** A text box where the student (or teacher) types what they want to be when they grow up. Examples: "Doctor," "Teacher," "Astronaut," "Pilot," "Firefighter," "Dancer." The teacher helps the younger students type this because small children may not know how to spell. The teacher can ask the student "What do you want to be when you grow up?" and type their answer.

### My Friends

- **Friends List:** This shows small name badges (like stickers) of the student's friends. Each badge is a small colored rectangle with the friend's name written inside.
- **How to add a friend:** There is an **"Add Friend"** button. When you click it, a small box pops up in the middle of the screen (this is called a "Sweet Alert" popup). The popup says "Enter friend's name" and has a text box. You type the friend's name (e.g., "Riya") and click the OK button. The popup closes and the friend's name appears as a new badge on the page. The name is shown in a random color (one time it might be pink, next time blue).
- **How to remove a friend:** Each badge has a little X or cross mark (✕) on it. Click the X. A popup appears asking "Are you sure you want to remove this friend?" Click "Yes" and the badge disappears. Click "No" and the badge stays.
- **Example:** If the student has 3 friends — Riya, Aarav, and Simran — each name shows as a small colored badge. Riya's badge might be pink, Aarav's badge might be blue, and Simran's badge might be green.

### My Favorites

These are dropdown selectors. Each one has a list of options. Click the dropdown arrow and pick one.

- **Favorite Color:** Options: Red, Blue, Green, Yellow, Pink, Orange, Purple, White, Black. The student clicks one.
- **Favorite Food:** Options: Pizza, Biryani, Noodles, Ice Cream, Dosa, Burger, Pasta, Rice, Roti.
- **Favorite Animal:** Options: Dog, Cat, Lion, Elephant, Rabbit, Horse, Cow, Monkey, Bird.
- **Favorite Flower:** Options: Rose, Lotus, Sunflower, Marigold, Lilly, Daisy, Jasmine.
- **Favorite Sport:** Options: Cricket, Football, Badminton, Kabaddi, Kho-Kho, Hockey, Tennis.
- **Favorite Subject:** Options: English, Hindi, Math, Science, Art, Music, Games.

## Page 3 — Goals & Assessment (Domain 1)

This page is where the teacher formally assesses the student's learning in the first domain (subject area). It has several parts stacked one below the other. Think of it like a test paper with multiple sections.

### Domain Title

- **What it is:** A bold heading at the top of the page that tells you which learning area this is. Example: "Language and Literacy Development" or "Cognitive Development" or "Social and Emotional Development" or "Physical Development" or "Creative Arts and Aesthetic Development."
- **How it works:** The domain title comes from the system settings. The teacher does not type it — it just appears automatically based on the student's grade level and the school's curriculum.

### Curricular Goals

- **What it is:** A list of big-picture goals for this domain. These are like the main targets for what the student should learn.
- **Example for "Language and Literacy Development":**
  - Goal 1: "The child develops listening and speaking skills in their home language."
  - Goal 2: "The child develops reading skills and print awareness."
  - Goal 3: "The child develops writing skills to express thoughts."
- **How it works:** These goals are already loaded from the system settings. The teacher reads them to understand what the student should be working toward. They cannot change the goals — they come from the government curriculum.

### Competencies

- **What it is:** More specific skills the student should be able to do. Competencies break each goal into smaller, measurable steps. Think of a goal like "Learn to cook" and competencies like "Can boil water," "Can chop vegetables," "Can use a pan."
- **Example for Goal 1 (listening and speaking):**
  - Competency 1: "Follows simple instructions in the classroom"
  - Competency 2: "Recites poems or rhymes with expression"
  - Competency 3: "Asks questions to satisfy curiosity"
  - Competency 4: "Describes familiar objects and events"

### Learning Activity

- **What it is:** A suggested activity description. This tells the teacher what kind of activity can help teach this skill. It is like a teaching tip.
- **Example:** "Ask the child to describe what they see in a picture book. Encourage them to use full sentences. Show them pictures of animals and ask them to name each one."
- **How it works:** This is just information for the teacher. It is not something you fill in. You read it to get ideas for classroom activities.

### Assessment Questions

- **What it is:** Questions the teacher can ask the student to see if they have learned the skill.
- **Example:** "Show the child a picture of a cat and a dog. Ask: Which animal says meow? Then ask: Which animal says bark?"
- **How it works:** These are questions from the system. The teacher can use them during assessment but does not type anything here. The teacher reads the question to the student and observes their answer.

### ASC Rubric Table

- **What it is:** A table that helps the teacher rate the student's performance. ASC stands for three levels of achievement:
  - **Stream (Beginning):** The student is just starting to learn this skill. They need a lot of help from the teacher. They cannot do it on their own yet.
  - **Mountain (Developing):** The student is making progress. They can do it sometimes but still need help or make mistakes.
  - **Sky (Achieved):** The student can do this skill on their own, without any help. They are confident and correct.

- **How it looks:** A table with rows for each competency or criteria, and columns for Stream, Mountain, and Sky. Each cell has a radio button (a small empty circle you click). When you click a circle, it fills with a dot to show it is selected. You can select only one circle per row.

- **Example table for "Language and Literacy Development":**

| Criteria | Stream (Beginning) | Mountain (Developing) | Sky (Achieved) |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------|----------------|
| Follows simple instructions | O | O | O |
| Speaks in short sentences | O | O | O |
| Recites rhymes with expression | O | O | O |
| Identifies letters of the alphabet | O | O | O |

- **How to fill it:** Read each row. Think about the student. Click the circle under the level that matches the student's ability.
  - For "Follows simple instructions": If the student can follow 2-step instructions easily (like "Pick up the book and put it on the table"), click Sky. If they can only follow 1-step instructions (like "Sit down") and need reminders, click Stream. If they can do both sometimes, click Mountain.
  - For "Recites rhymes with expression": If the student can recite a full rhyme with proper expression and actions, click Sky. If they can say parts of a rhyme but forget some words, click Mountain. If they cannot recite any rhyme yet, click Stream.

## Pages 4 to 18 — Subject / Domain Pages (Dynamically Generated Pages)

### How These Pages Work

Pages 4 through 18 are not the same every time. Their content is loaded from something called a **template configuration** — a set of instructions stored in the database that tells the system which sections and fields to show. Think of it like a recipe: the template says "show a text box for Teacher Comments, a dropdown for Grade, and a yes/no toggle for 'Completed.'" The system reads the recipe and builds each page automatically.

The school admin or the curriculum coordinator decides what goes on each page. The teacher just fills the fields that appear. The pages cover different learning areas like:
- Page 4: Feedback & Self-Assessment
- Page 5: Physical Development (motor skills, health habits)
- Page 6: Creative Arts (drawing, painting, craft)
- Page 7: Math Readiness (numbers, shapes, counting)
- Page 8: Language Skills (reading readiness, writing readiness)
- Pages 9-18: More learning areas as decided by the school

### What Each Page Looks Like

Each page has the same overall structure:
1. **Page Title:** At the top of the page, written in bold letters. Example: "Physical Development" or "Creative Arts" or "Math Readiness."
2. **Sections:** Each page can have one or more sections. Sections group related fields together. A section has its own heading (like "Gross Motor Skills" or "Fine Motor Skills") and a colored background or border to separate it from other sections.
3. **Three-Column Layout:** Fields are arranged in 3 columns side by side, like a grid. This helps fit more information on the screen without scrolling too much. You read from left to right, top to bottom — like reading a newspaper with three columns.

### Types of Fields You Will See on These Pages

Here is every kind of field that can appear, with examples and step-by-step instructions:

**1. Text Input (Short Answer)**
- **What it is:** A single line where you type words. It looks like an empty box with a blinking line inside.
- **Example label:** "Teacher's Observation:" — the teacher types something like "Riya enjoys drawing and shows good hand control."
- **How to fill:** Click inside the box with your mouse. The box gets a blue border to show it is active. Type your text using the keyboard. To correct a mistake, press Backspace to delete letters.
- **Real example:** The label says "Comment on student's participation." The teacher types: "Aarav participates actively in group activities and helps his friends."

**2. Number Input**
- **What it is:** A box where you type only numbers. If you try to type letters, nothing happens.
- **Example label:** "Number of words read correctly:" — the teacher types "15" (not "fifteen").
- **How to fill:** Click inside the box. Type digits (0-9). Use the numbers at the top of the keyboard or the number pad on the right side.
- **Real example:** The label says "Score out of 50." The teacher types "42."

**3. Dropdown / Select**
- **What it is:** A box with a small downward arrow (▼) on the right side. When you click the arrow, a list of options drops down. You pick one.
- **Example label:** "Overall Grade:" — options are A (Excellent), B (Good), C (Needs Improvement), D (Not Assessed).
- **How to fill:** Click the arrow. The list opens. Scroll up or down with your mouse wheel or by clicking and dragging the scroll bar. Click the option you want. The list closes and your choice shows in the box.
- **Real example:** The label says "Art Skill Level." Options: Beginning, Developing, Proficient, Advanced. The teacher picks "Proficient."

**4. Yes / No Toggle (Boolean)**
- **What it is:** A switch or two small circles — one says "Yes" and one says "No." You click one. Sometimes it looks like a light switch that you slide left (No) or right (Yes).
- **Example label:** "Can identify colors:" — the teacher clicks "Yes" if the student can identify basic colors, or "No" if they cannot.
- **How to fill:** Just click the word "Yes" or the circle next to it. The selected option gets highlighted. The other option becomes grey.
- **Real example:** Label says "Completed the activity." If the student finished, click Yes. If not, click No.

**5. Checkbox**
- **What it is:** A small square box you click to put a checkmark (✓) inside. You can check more than one box in a group.
- **Example label:** "Skills Observed:" — boxes for ☐ Listening, ☐ Speaking, ☐ Reading, ☐ Writing. The teacher checks all that apply.
- **How to fill:** Click the box. A checkmark appears inside. Click again to remove the checkmark.
- **Real example:** Label says "Areas of improvement:" with boxes for ☐ Handwriting, ☐ Reading fluency, ☐ Math facts, ☐ Attention span. The teacher checks all the areas the student needs to improve.

**6. Image Upload**
- **What it is:** A button that says "Upload Image" or "Choose File." Click it and pick a picture from your computer.
- **Example label:** "Upload student artwork:" — the teacher takes a photo of the student's drawing with a phone, transfers it to the computer, and uploads it.
- **How to fill:** Click the "Upload" button. A window opens showing your computer's files. Navigate to the folder where the picture is saved. Click the picture file (it will have a name like "Ravi_drawing.jpg"). Click the "Open" button. The window closes and the picture appears on the form as a small thumbnail (miniature version). A "Remove" button also appears in case you want to remove the picture and upload a different one.
- **Important:** The picture must be in JPG or PNG format. These are the most common picture formats. The file size should not be too large (the system may reject very big files over 5 MB).

**7. File Upload**
- **What it is:** Same as image upload but for any type of file (PDF, Word document, etc.).
- **Example label:** "Upload worksheet:" — the teacher uploads a scanned copy of the student's completed worksheet saved as a PDF.
- **How to fill:** Same steps as image upload. Click "Upload," find the file, click "Open."

**8. JSON / Structured Data Field**
- **What it is:** A special field that stores information in a computer-friendly format. You usually do not see the raw JSON — instead, the system shows you a simple form that saves as JSON in the background. Think of it like a box that holds multiple pieces of information at once.
- **Example:** "Competency Mapping" — the teacher picks a competency from one dropdown (e.g., "Follows directions") and a level from another dropdown (e.g., "Developing"). The system saves this as structured data behind the scenes.
- **How to fill:** You fill in the simple form fields shown on screen. You do not need to understand or touch JSON.

### How to Reorder Sections (Drag and Drop)

- Each section has a small icon on the left side that looks like 6 dots (⠿ or ≡) or a grid. This is called a **drag handle**.
- **How to use it:** Move your mouse over the 6 dots icon. The cursor changes from an arrow to a hand or cross shape. Click and hold the left mouse button on the icon. While holding the button, move your mouse up or down. The section follows your mouse — it slides up or down. When you reach the position where you want the section, let go of the mouse button. The section stays in its new place. The other sections automatically move to make room.
- **Why use it:** If the teacher wants "Creative Arts" to appear before "Physical Development," they can drag the section up. This only changes the order on the screen — it does not change any data or delete anything.
- **Important:** Only sections within the same page can be reordered. You cannot drag a section from Page 4 to Page 6. Each page has its own sections.

### How to Edit Labels

- **What is a label?** The text next to each field that tells you what it is (e.g., "Student Name," "Grade," "Comments"). Labels are like name tags for each field.
- **How to edit:** Double-click (click twice fast) on any label. The text turns into a text box with a blue border. Type the new label text (e.g., change "Teacher Observation" to "Teacher Remarks"). Press the Enter key on your keyboard. The text box turns back into a regular label with your new text.
- **Why use it:** If the school wants to use different names for fields, they can change them without needing a programmer. For example, if the school prefers "Instructor Notes" instead of "Teacher Comments," they can edit the label.
- **Caution:** Editing the label changes it for ALL students, not just this one student. So be careful — you are changing the form itself.

## Page 4 (Example) — Feedback & Self-Assessment

This is a specific page that usually appears at position 4. It contains:
- **Observational Notes:** A large text box where the teacher types notes about the student's overall progress. This box is bigger than the short text boxes — it can hold multiple sentences or even a paragraph. Example: "Aarav has improved in sharing toys with classmates. He still needs help with holding a pencil correctly. He enjoys story time and asks many questions."
- **Student Self-Assessment Questions:** Simple questions the teacher asks the student, like "What did you enjoy learning this term?" or "What was hard for you?" The teacher types the student's answers in text boxes. This helps the student feel involved in their own learning.
- **Work Habits Assessment:** Dropdowns where the teacher rates things like "Participates in class activities" (Always/Sometimes/Rarely), "Completes work on time," "Follows classroom rules."

## Key Features — Summary

### Save Button
- **Where it is:** At the very bottom of the page, below all the tabs. You may need to scroll down to reach it. It is a rectangular button that says "Save" or "Save All."
- **What it does:** Saves ALL 18 pages at once. You do not need to save each page separately. When you click Save, the computer sends all the information you typed on ALL pages to the database.
- **How to use it:** Click the Save button with your mouse. A message appears at the top or center of the screen saying "Saved successfully!" or "Data saved successfully." If you forgot to fill a required field (a field that must be filled), the system highlights it in red and stops saving until you fill it. The message will say "Please fill all required fields."
- **Important:** Always click Save before closing the page or moving to a different student's card, or your work will be lost. If you close the browser tab or the window without saving, everything you typed will be gone. The system does NOT auto-save — you must click the button yourself.
- **Tip:** Get into the habit of clicking Save every 10-15 minutes while working.

### Auto-Fill
- Most fields on Pages 1 and 2 fill themselves from data already in the system (student records, school info, attendance system).
- The teacher does not type student names, parent names, school name, etc. They just check if it is correct.
- Attendance percentages are calculated automatically. The teacher only types total working days and present days.
- If the school uses the attendance module (a separate system), even the working days and present days may be filled automatically.

### Drag and Drop Reordering
- You can reorder sections within a page by dragging the drag handle (6 dots icon).
- Hold the left mouse button on the dots, move up or down, release.
- This only changes the display order. Data is not affected.

### Editable Labels
- Double-click any field label to edit it.
- Type the new label and press Enter.
- This lets schools customize field names without needing a programmer.

### PDF Preview
- A button at the bottom or top that says "Preview PDF" or "Generate PDF."
- Click it to see what the printed Holistic Progress Card will look like.
- The PDF shows all the information in a nice, print-ready format with the school logo and student details.
- You can print the PDF or save it to your computer.

### Attendance Table Controls
- **Add a row:** Click the **+** (plus) button at the bottom of the table. A new empty row appears.
- **Remove a row:** Click the **-** (minus) button next to the row. A popup asks "Are you sure you want to delete this row?" Click Yes.
- **Add a column:** In some versions, there is a button to add new columns (like "Remarks" or "Verified By").

## Example Scenario — How a Teacher Uses This Card

**Example:** Mrs. Sharma is the class teacher for UKG. She needs to fill the Holistic Progress Card for a student named Ravi Kumar.

Mrs. Sharma opens the computer and logs into the school system. She clicks on "Teacher Cards" and selects "Foundation Template." She searches for Ravi Kumar's name and clicks on his card.

**On Page 1:** She sees Ravi's name already filled in — "Ravi Kumar." She sees the school name "Delhi Public School" already there. She checks that the admission number matches Ravi's records. It says "ADM-2024-0089." She scrolls to the Attendance Table and sees that April has 22 working days and Ravi was present 20 days (90.9%). She writes in the reason for absence: "Was sick with fever for 2 days." She scrolls to check the parent details. Mother's name: "Sunita Devi." Father's name: "Rajesh Kumar." Both look correct. She does not need to change anything.

**On Page 2:** She turns the computer screen so Ravi can see it. She asks Ravi "Are you a boy or a girl?" Ravi says "Boy." Ravi reaches out and clicks the boy emoji 👦. It turns blue. Mrs. Sharma asks "How old are you, Ravi?" Ravi says "Five." She clicks the age dropdown and selects "5." She asks "What do you want to be when you grow up?" Ravi says "Pilot!" She types "Pilot" in the dream job box. She asks "Who lives at home with you?" Ravi says "Mummy, Papa, and my didi (sister)." Ravi clicks the woman, man, and girl emojis. All three turn blue. She asks "Who are your friends?" Ravi says "Aarav and Simran." She clicks "Add Friend" and types "Aarav." A pink badge appears. She types "Simran" and a blue badge appears.

**On Page 3:** She sees the domain title "Language and Literacy Development." She reads the goals and competencies. She looks at the criteria "Follows simple instructions" and thinks about Ravi. Ravi can follow 2-step instructions like "Put your book away and wash your hands." She clicks the "Sky" circle. For "Recites rhymes," Ravi knows "Twinkle Twinkle" but forgets some words. She clicks "Mountain."

**Pages 4 to 18:** Mrs. Sharma scrolls through the remaining pages. On the "Physical Development" page, she types "Ravi can hop on one foot but struggles with skipping" in the Teacher Observation text box. On the "Math Readiness" page, she sees a Yes/No toggle for "Can count from 1 to 20." She clicks "Yes." She scrolls to the "Creative Arts" page and sees an Image Upload field. She has a photo of Ravi's drawing on her computer. She clicks "Upload," finds the photo, and uploads it.

**After finishing all 18 pages:** Mrs. Sharma scrolls to the bottom of the page and clicks the "Save" button. A message appears: "Saved successfully!" She closes the card and moves to the next student.
