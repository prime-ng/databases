# HPC Teacher Card — Secondary Template (BV4)
**For grades:** 11th, 12th
**Total Pages:** 44 pages (tabs)

## What is this?

This is the teacher data entry card for students in Classes 11 and 12 — the final two years of school. It has 44 pages and includes everything from the Middle template plus special features for senior students.

At this level, students choose a **stream** (Science, Commerce, or Arts). The card automatically shows only the subjects for that student's stream. For example, a Science student sees Physics, Chemistry, and Biology pages. A Commerce student sees Accountancy, Business Studies, and Economics pages. The school does not need to create different forms for different streams — the system handles it automatically.

This card also includes **NCrF credits** (a national credit system that tracks learning like a bank balance), a **Goals & Aspirations wizard** (a step-by-step form for career planning), and stream-specific rubrics.

Think of this as the final, most complete school report. It captures everything a student has learned in their senior years. If you printed all 44 pages, it would be a thick document showing the student's entire senior secondary journey.

## Page 1 — School & Student Information

This page is the same as in Foundation, Preparatory, and Middle templates. Most fields auto-fill from the school's computer system.

### School Details (auto-filled)
- **School Name:** The full school name (e.g., "Delhi Public School"). Comes from school registration.
- **Address:** Street address of the school.
- **Village / Town:** Location details.
- **BRC, CRC:** Government education office codes.
- **State:** Where the school is located.
- **Pincode (6 boxes):** Type one digit per box (e.g., 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1).
- **UDISE Code (11 boxes):** Government school ID number — one digit per box.
- **Medium of Instruction:** Teaching language (e.g., "English").
- **Rural/Urban:** Whether the school is in a village or city.
- **Affiliation Number:** School's board affiliation (CBSE, ICSE, etc.).
- **Email, Website, Phone Numbers:** Contact information.

### Student Details (auto-filled from admission records)
- **Full Name:** First, middle, last name. Comes from the admission form.
- **Admission Number:** Unique student ID (e.g., "ADM-2023-0012").
- **Roll Number:** Class roll number (e.g., "28").
- **Gender:** Boy or girl.
- **Date of Birth:** Shows as dd/mm/yyyy (e.g., "23/01/2007").
- **Class:** 11th or 12th.
- **Section:** A, B, C, etc.
- **House:** Red, Blue, Green, Yellow, etc.
- **Stream:** Science, Commerce, or Arts (Humanities). This is important because it decides which subject pages appear.
- **Aadhar ID:** 12-digit government ID. Twelve separate boxes.
- **APAAR ID:** Education ID. Separate digit boxes.
- **Birth Certificate Number:** From the birth certificate.
- **Student Photo:** Space for the student's picture. Click "Upload" to add one.

### Parent / Guardian Details (auto-filled)
- Father's name, mother's name, guardian's name
- Phone numbers (father, mother, guardian)
- Occupations and email addresses

### Contact & Demographics (auto-filled)
- Mobile number, email, home address, home pincode
- Religion, caste, nationality, mother tongue, blood group, EWS status

### Physical Measurements (auto-filled)
- Height in centimeters (e.g., "165 cm")
- Weight in kilograms (e.g., "55 kg")
These come from school health records.

### Attendance Table (same as other templates)
- Rows for April to March (12 months).
- **Total Working Days:** How many days school was open. Teacher types the number.
- **Days Present:** How many days the student attended. Teacher types the number.
- **Attendance %:** Calculated automatically (Present ÷ Working × 100).
- **Reason for Absence:** Text box for explaining long absences.
- **Add/Remove Rows:** Click **+** to add a row for a special period. Click **-** to delete a row (popup asks "Are you sure?").

## NCrF Credits — National Credit Framework

### What Are NCrF Credits?

NCrF stands for **National Credit Framework**. It is a government system that gives students "credits" (points) for everything they learn — both school subjects and other activities like sports, art, community service, and vocational training.

Think of credits like money in a bank account. Every subject and activity the student does adds credits to their account. At the end of the year, the total credits show how much the student has learned.

### Why Credits Matter

- Credits are recognized across all schools in India under NEP 2020.
- Students can carry their credits if they change schools or move to a different city.
- Credits can be used later in college, university, or vocational training.
- If a student earns enough credits, they may not need to repeat certain subjects if they switch streams or courses.

### How Credits Are Calculated

The system calculates credits automatically based on:
- **Grade Level:** Class 11 gives a certain number of base credits. Class 12 gives another set. The government sets these numbers (for example, Class 11 might give 40 total credits for the year).
- **Each Subject:** Every subject gives a specific number of credits. For example:
  - Physics (Theory): 5 credits
  - Physics (Practical): 2 credits
  - Chemistry (Theory): 5 credits
  - Chemistry (Practical): 2 credits
  - Mathematics: 5 credits
  - English: 4 credits
  - Physical Education: 2 credits
- **Activities:** Non-academic activities also give credits:
  - Sports participation: 1-2 credits
  - Community service: 1 credit
  - Art/Music/Dance: 1-2 credits
  - Vocational training: 2-3 credits
- **Total:** All credits are added together to give the student's total credits for the year.

### Where Credits Appear on the Card

A special page or section called **NCrF Credit Summary** shows:
- A table listing each subject with its credits (e.g., "Physics: 5 credits, Chemistry: 5 credits")
- A table for activity credits (e.g., "Sports: 2 credits, Community Service: 1 credit")
- Total credits earned this year
- Credits earned in previous years (if the school tracks this)
- Credits still needed to reach the minimum requirement

### Example of a Completed NCrF Credit Summary

| Subject | Credits |
|---------|---------|
| Physics (Theory) | 5 |
| Physics (Practical) | 2 |
| Chemistry (Theory) | 5 |
| Chemistry (Practical) | 2 |
| Mathematics | 5 |
| English | 4 |
| Physical Education | 2 |
| Life Skills | 2 |
| Community Service | 1 |
| **Total** | **28** |

### Can the School Change Credits?

- The system auto-calculates credits from the grade level and subjects. These numbers come from government guidelines.
- If the school needs to adjust credits (for a special program or curriculum), a school admin can override the values in the system settings.
- Teachers normally do NOT change credits. They just see the values displayed on the card.

## Goals & Aspirations Wizard

### What Is This?

A special section (one or more pages) where the student types their career and life goals. At the senior secondary level, students are actively thinking about college and careers. This section helps them plan their future and lets the teacher provide guidance.

### What the Student Fills

The student answers questions one by one (like a "wizard" — a step-by-step form). The questions include:

- **Career Goal:** What career do you want to pursue? (e.g., "Software Engineer," "Doctor," "Business Owner," "Lawyer," "Architect")
- **College / University Plans:** Which colleges or universities are you interested in? (e.g., "IIT Delhi," "Delhi University," "National Institute of Design," "AIIMS")
- **Course / Degree Interest:** What do you want to study? (e.g., "B.Tech in Computer Science," "MBBS," "B.Com (Hons)", "BA in Economics")
- **Skill Development Targets:** What skills do you want to learn? (e.g., "Public speaking," "Coding in Python," "Financial analysis," "Creative writing")
- **Short-term Goals (this year):** What do you want to achieve in Class 11 or 12? (e.g., "Score 90% in Physics," "Win the debate competition," "Complete a coding certificate course")
- **Long-term Goals (5 years):** Where do you see yourself in 5 years? (e.g., "Working as a software developer at a top tech company," "Running my own business," "Pursuing MD after MBBS")

### How the Wizard Works

1. **Student fills the form:** The student logs into their own portal (a separate website for students, not the teacher's card). They open the Goals & Aspirations form.
2. **Step-by-step:** The form shows one question at a time. The student types their answer and clicks "Next." They cannot skip questions — each one must be answered before moving forward.
3. **Auto-save:** After answering each question, the data saves automatically. The student can close the form and come back later to continue.
4. **Data appears in teacher's card:** When the teacher opens the student's Teacher Card, the student's answers are already filled in on the Goals & Aspirations page. The teacher can read them immediately.
5. **Teacher adds feedback:** Below each of the student's answers, there is a text box labeled "Teacher Comment" or "Teacher Advice." The teacher types their feedback, suggestions, or encouragement.
6. **Endorsement:** The teacher can click an "Endorse" or "Acknowledge" button next to each goal to show they have read and support it.

### Example of a Completed Goals Section

> **Student:** Priya Sharma (Class 12, Science)
> 
> **Career Goal:** Software Engineer
> *Teacher Comment:* "Great choice, Priya. Your strong performance in Computer Science supports this goal."
> 
> **College Interest:** IIT Bombay, BITS Pilani
> *Teacher Comment:* "Both are excellent choices. Focus on JEE Advanced preparation."
> 
> **Skill Targets:** Learn Python, improve public speaking, build a portfolio project
> *Teacher Comment:* "I recommend joining the coding club. We have a Python workshop next month."
> 
> **Short-term Goal:** Score 90% in Computer Science
> *Teacher Comment:* "You are currently at 85%. With focused effort, 90% is achievable."
> 
> **Long-term Goal:** Work as a software developer at Google or Microsoft
> *Teacher Comment:* "An ambitious goal! Start building your skills now with small projects."
> 
> *Endorsed by: Mr. Patel (Class Teacher) on 15/01/2026*

### How the Teacher Interacts with This Page

- **Read:** The teacher reads what the student has written. This helps the teacher understand the student's aspirations.
- **Comment:** The teacher types feedback in the text boxes below each goal. Comments can be encouraging, advisory, or constructive.
- **Endorse:** Click "Endorse" to formally acknowledge the goal. The endorsement shows the teacher's name and the date.
- **Discuss:** The teacher can use the goals as a conversation starter during parent-teacher meetings or one-on-one student meetings.

## Multi-Source Assessment (Same as Middle)

The Secondary template also includes assessment data from multiple sources:

### 1. Student Self-Assessment
- Students rate themselves on rubrics for each subject.
- Their ratings appear next to the teacher's ratings in a different color (e.g., blue for student, black for teacher).
- The teacher can compare and discuss any differences with the student.

### 2. Parent Input
- Parents can submit observations about study habits, career discussions, health, and behavior at home.
- Parent notes appear in a separate section labeled "Parent Observation" with the parent's name.
- Teacher cannot edit parent notes — they are read-only.

### 3. Peer Review Results
- Classmates give anonymous feedback on collaboration, participation, and teamwork.
- Only average scores and summarized comments appear (no names shown).

### 4. LMS Data Integration
- Exam scores, quiz results, and assignment grades from the Learning Management System auto-fill.
- These appear in grey (read-only) boxes. Teacher cannot edit them.
- If there is no LMS, fields stay empty for manual entry.

## PARAKH Framework Alignment

PARAKH stands for **Performance Assessment, Review, and Analysis of Knowledge for Holistic Development**. It is a national assessment body under NEP 2020 that sets standards for student assessment.

### What This Means for the Teacher

- The rubrics, grading scales, and assessment criteria on the Secondary template are already aligned with PARAKH standards.
- The teacher does not need to do anything extra — the rubrics come pre-aligned from the template.
- The report card format is recognized by government bodies across India.
- Schools can submit PARAKH-compliant data if required by the education department.

### Why This Matters

- It ensures consistency in assessment across all schools following NEP 2020.
- The student's report card is valid and recognized everywhere.
- It makes it easier for students to move between schools — their assessment data is in a standard format.

## Key Features

### Save Button
- At the bottom of the page. Saves ALL 44 pages at once.
- Click Save. A green banner says "Saved successfully." If required fields are empty, they turn red and the save stops.
- **Always click Save before closing the card.** If you close the browser tab or window without saving, all work on all 44 pages is lost.

### Auto-Fill
- School information, student information, attendance percentages, LMS exam scores, student self-assessment answers, peer review averages, parent observations, and NCrF credits all fill automatically.
- The teacher mainly fills rubric ratings, comments, and final grades.
- The Goals & Aspirations section is filled by the student — the teacher adds comments.

### Drag and Drop Reordering
- Each section has a drag handle (⠿ icon) on the left.
- Click and hold the icon, move up or down, then release.
- This reorders sections within a page. It does not delete data.

### Editable Labels
- Double-click any field label to rename it.
- Type the new name and press Enter.
- Schools can customize field names without needing a programmer.

### PDF Preview
- "Preview PDF" button shows a print-ready version of all 44 pages.
- Shows school logo, student information, all assessments, NCrF credits, and goals.
- You can print the PDF or save it to your computer.

## Example Scenario — How a Teacher Uses the Secondary Card

**Example:** Rohan is in Class 12 Science. His teacher, Mr. Patel, needs to fill his Holistic Progress Card.

Mr. Patel logs into the school system and opens the Secondary Template. He searches for Rohan Sharma and opens his card.

**Page 1:** Before filling anything, Mr. Patel checks the information. Rohan's name, admission number, and stream (Science) are correct. The attendance table shows Rohan has 92% attendance overall. Mr. Patel notices the NCrF Credit Summary box on the side shows 28 credits so far for Class 12. He does not need to change this — it is auto-calculated.

**Page 2:** Rohan has already filled his profile on the student portal. His dream job is "Software Engineer." His favorite subject is "Computer Science." Mr. Patel reads this and makes a mental note.

**Page 3-6 (Physics):** Mr. Patel fills the ASC rubric for Physics. For "Derives physics formulas," Rohan is excellent — clicks "Peak." For "Sets up experimental apparatus," Rohan is good but sometimes needs guidance — clicks "Sky." The LMS has already filled Rohan's Physics theory score: 88/100. The practical score: 28/30. Mr. Patel adds a comment: "Excellent understanding of concepts. Continue working on numerical problem speed."

**Pages 7-10 (Chemistry):** Mr. Patel fills the Chemistry rubric. He sees the student self-assessment column — Rohan rated himself "Sky" for "Organic chemistry reactions" but Mr. Patel knows Rohan got 70/100 in the organic chemistry test. Mr. Patel clicks "Mountain" in the teacher column and writes in his comment: "Rohan, your self-assessment is higher than your test score suggests. Let's discuss strategies to improve organic chemistry understanding."

**Pages 35-36 (Goals & Aspirations):** Mr. Patel clicks on this page. He sees Rohan's career goal: "I want to be a software engineer at Google." College interest: "IIT Delhi, BITS Pilani." Skill targets: "Learn Python, improve public speaking." Mr. Patel types his comments: "Great goal, Rohan. Focus on JEE preparation. I recommend joining the coding club and participating in the upcoming hackathon." He clicks "Endorse" next to the career goal.

**Pages 37-38 (NCrF Credit Summary):** Mr. Patel reviews the credits. Physics: 7 credits (5 theory + 2 practical). Chemistry: 7 credits. Mathematics: 5 credits. English: 4 credits. Computer Science: 5 credits. Total: 28 credits. All correct.

**After all 44 pages:** Mr. Patel scrolls to the bottom and clicks Save. "Saved successfully!" He closes Rohan's card and moves to the next student.
