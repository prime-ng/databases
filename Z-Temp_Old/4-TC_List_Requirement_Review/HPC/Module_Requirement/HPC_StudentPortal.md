# HPC Student Portal – Complete Guide for Students and Staff

**Who can use this portal:** Students who have a school login and have been given a special permission called `tenant.hpc-student.*`. If a student cannot see this portal, the school's system administrator needs to check their permissions.

**Which students can use it:** Only students in **Middle stage (Classes 6 to 10)** and **Secondary stage (Classes 11 and 12)**. Younger students (Nursery to Class 5 – Foundation and Preparatory stages) do NOT use this portal. Their cards are filled entirely by teachers and parents. This is because younger children may not be able to assess themselves reliably.

**What this feature does:** The Student Portal lets students contribute their own voice to their Holistic Progress Card. Instead of the teacher being the ONLY person assessing the student, the student gets to share their own thoughts about their learning, their goals, and their classmates. This follows the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 "multi-contributor" model. This means the student, teacher, parent, and peers all contribute to a complete picture of the student's development. No single person has all the information – together, they create the full picture.

**What students can do here – a quick overview:**

1. **View their dashboard** – See a list of all their cards and their completion status.
2. **Fill in a self-assessment** – Write about what they learned, their strengths, their weaknesses, and rate themselves.
3. **Set goals and aspirations** (Secondary only – Classes 11 and 12) – A step-by-step wizard to plan their career, college, and skill goals.
4. **Review their classmates** (peer assessment) – Give feedback on their peers' performance and behaviour.
5. **Submit their work** – Lock their answers so the teacher can review them.

## Feature 1: Student Dashboard (The Main Screen)

When a student logs into the portal using their school username and password, they see a **dashboard**. The dashboard is the first screen after login. It shows a list of all the Holistic Progress Cards that need their attention.

**Think of the dashboard like a to-do list.** Each card is an assignment that the student needs to complete. The student can see at a glance which cards are done, which are still being worked on, and which have not been started yet.

**What each card on the dashboard shows:**

| Information | What It Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Class & Section** | Which class and section the card belongs to. This helps the student identify the correct card if they study in multiple classes or have cards from previous terms. | "Class 10-A", "Class 11-B" |
| **Term** | Which academic term the card is for. This tells the student which term's self-assessment needs to be filled. | "Term 1 (April – September 2025)", "Term 2 (October – March 2026)" |
| **Completion Percentage** | A number showing how much of the student's part is done. If the student has filled 3 out of 5 sections, this shows 60%. The percentage is calculated automatically by the system. | 0% (not started), 50% (half done), 100% (completely filled) |
| **Status** | One of three statuses (see table below). This tells the student whether they need to take action or not. | Pending, In Progress, Completed |

**Card status for students – what each means:**

| Status | What It Means | Can the Student Edit? |
|---|---|---|
| **Pending** | The student has NOT started working on this card yet. The sections are all empty. The student has not clicked any buttons or typed any text. This is the starting status for every new card. | Yes – the student can open the card and start filling it at any time. |
| **In Progress** | The student HAS started working on this card. Some sections are filled, some are still empty. The student has not clicked "Submit" yet. This means they can still go back and change their answers. | Yes – the student can open the card, edit answers, and save changes. |
| **Completed** | The student has clicked the "Submit" button. Their part is finished. The card is locked. The student can see their answers but cannot change them. This status tells the teacher that the student's part is ready for review. | No – the card is locked. The student cannot make any more changes. |

**What the student does next:** They click on a card to open it and start filling in their sections. The dashboard is just the starting point – the real work happens after they click a card.

## Feature 2: Self-Assessment Form

When the student clicks on a card from the dashboard, they see the **Self-Assessment Form**. This form shows only the fields that are meant for students. All teacher fields, parent fields, and peer fields are hidden. The student cannot see them or change them. This is important – it means the student cannot accidentally see the teacher's comments about them, or the parent's observations.

**What the student fills in – section by section:**

### Self-Reflection (Text Box)

This is a large text box where the student writes their thoughts about their own learning during the term. It is completely free text – the student can write whatever they want. There is no right or wrong answer.

**Questions the student is encouraged to think about:**
- "What did I learn this term?"
- "What subjects did I enjoy the most?"
- "What was the most challenging thing I faced?"
- "How did I overcome my challenges?"
- "What am I proud of?"

**Example of what a student might write:**
> "I think I did well in Maths this term because I practised every day. I used to struggle with fractions but now I can solve them easily. I need to work harder on my English essays because my teacher said I need to improve my grammar. I really enjoyed the science experiments, especially the one about plant growth. Next term, I want to participate more in class discussions."

### My Strengths (Text Box)

The student identifies what they are good at. This can be academic subjects, skills, or personal qualities.

**Example:** "I am good at solving maths problems. I am a good listener and my friends come to me when they need help. I am good at drawing and painting."

### Areas for Improvement (Text Box)

The student identifies what they want to get better at. This helps the teacher understand where the student needs support.

**Example:** "I want to improve my handwriting because my teacher sometimes cannot read what I write. I also want to get better at public speaking because I get nervous when I have to present in front of the class."

### Learning Preferences (Text, Drop-down, or Checkboxes)

The student indicates how they learn best. The format depends on how the school admin configured the template. It could be:

- **A text box:** "I learn best when..."
- **A drop-down menu:** Options like "By watching videos", "By reading books", "By doing hands-on activities", "By listening to the teacher"
- **Checkboxes:** The student can select multiple options that apply

**Example:** "I learn best when I can see things visually. Diagrams and charts help me understand better than just reading text. I also learn well when I work in a group because my friends explain things to me."

### Self-Rating (Grade or Descriptor Selection)

The student rates their own progress for each subject or skill. They use the SAME scale that the teacher uses in the card. This is not a simple "good or bad" choice – it uses the rubric levels defined in the template.

**Example:** For the subject "English", the student might see these levels:

| Level | Meaning | Numeric Value |
|---|---|---|
| Excellent | The student excels in this area | 4 |
| Good | The student is doing well | 3 |
| Developing | The student is improving but needs more work | 2 |
| Needs Improvement | The student is struggling and needs help | 1 |

The student selects one level for each subject. For example, the student might select:

| Subject | Self-Rating |
|---|---|
| English | Good (3) |
| Mathematics | Excellent (4) |
| Science | Good (3) |
| Social Studies | Developing (2) |
| Art | Excellent (4) |

**Important rule:** The student can ONLY see and edit fields that are marked as "student-owned" in the template configuration. Everything else is invisible to them. The school admin decides which fields are student-owned when they create the template in Template Management.

## Feature 3: Goals & Aspirations Wizard (Only for Classes 11 and 12)

For older students (Class 11 and Class 12 – Secondary stage), there is a special guided form called a "wizard". A wizard is a step-by-step form. The student fills one screen at a time, then clicks "Next" to go to the next screen. It is like filling out a multi-page application form online.

**Why only for Classes 11 and 12?** Students in these classes are nearing the end of their school journey. They need to start thinking about their future – career, higher education, and life goals. The wizard helps them structure their thinking.

**What the wizard covers – page by page:**

### Page 1: Career Goals

The student writes about what profession they want to pursue after finishing school.

**Questions the student answers:**
- "What career are you interested in?"
- "Why does this career interest you?"
- "What do you know about this career?"

**Example:** "I want to become a software engineer. I am interested in this career because I love computers and I enjoy creating things. I know that software engineers design apps and websites. I have been learning basic coding on my own."

### Page 2: Higher Education Plans

The student writes about their college or university aspirations.

**Questions the student answers:**
- "What course or subject do you want to study after Class 12?"
- "Which colleges or universities are you interested in?"
- "What entrance exams do you need to take?"

**Example:** "I want to study Computer Science at IIT. I plan to take the JEE Advanced exam. I also want to apply to some private colleges as backup options. I need to score at least 90% in Class 12 to be eligible for the colleges I am interested in."

### Page 3: Skill Development Targets

The student identifies skills they want to build or improve. Skills are not just academic subjects – they include things like communication, teamwork, leadership, and technical skills.

**Questions the student answers:**
- "What skills do you want to develop this year?"
- "How will you develop these skills?"
- "How will you measure your progress?"

**Example:** "I want to improve my public speaking skills because I get nervous when presenting. I will practise by speaking in front of the mirror and volunteering to present in class. I will measure my progress by how comfortable I feel when presenting."

### Page 4: Short-Term Goals (For This Academic Year)

The student sets goals that they want to achieve by the end of the current academic year.

**Example:** "My short-term goals are:
1. Score above 85% in all subjects this year.
2. Win the inter-school debate competition.
3. Complete a basic Python programming course online.
4. Improve my handwriting."

### Page 5: Long-Term Goals (For the Next 5 Years)

The student looks further ahead and sets goals for the next 5 years.

**Example:** "My long-term goals are:
1. Graduate from a good engineering college.
2. Get a job at a top technology company like Google or Microsoft.
3. Start my own tech startup by the time I am 25.
4. Travel to at least 5 countries to learn about different cultures."

**Each goal has two parts:**
1. **Description** – What the goal is, written in the student's own words.
2. **Target Timeline** – When the student hopes to achieve it. Examples: "by the end of this term", "by the end of the year", "within the next 2 years", "by the time I finish college".

**Flexibility:** The student can come back and update their goals throughout the term. Goals are NOT locked until the card is submitted. This means the student can change their mind, add new goals, or remove old ones as they progress through the year.

**Saving and coming back later:** The wizard has a "Save and Continue Later" button at the bottom of every page. The student can save their progress, log out, and come back another day. When they return, the wizard takes them back to the exact page where they stopped.

**Teacher can view and comment:** The teacher can open the card and see everything the student has written in the wizard. The teacher can leave comments or suggestions. For example, the teacher might write: "Great goal! I know a good online course for Python. Let me share the link with you."

## Feature 4: Peer Assessment (Reviewing Classmates)

Students review their classmates as part of a structured peer evaluation process. This is NOT just "write a comment about your friend." It is a formal, structured process that follows rules to ensure fairness.

**Why peer assessment exists:**
- Students spend a lot of time with each other. They see things that the teacher might miss – like who helps others, who works well in a group, who is always prepared.
- Peer assessment teaches students to give and receive constructive feedback.
- It builds accountability and helps students reflect on their own behaviour.
- It gives the teacher a fuller picture of classroom dynamics.

### How Peer Assessment Works – Step by Step

**Step 1: Teacher sets up cycles**
In Template Management, the admin defines how many peer review cycles there are. A cycle is one round of reviews. There can be from 1 to 9 cycles per template. The teacher (not the admin) assigns which cycle each student participates in and when.

**Step 2: System assigns reviewers automatically**
The system pairs students up for each cycle. It follows two strict rules:

- **Rule 1 – No self-review:** No student can be asked to review themselves. The system will never assign you to review your own work.
- **Rule 2 – No reciprocal pairs:** If Student A reviews Student B, then Student B does NOT review Student A in the same cycle. This prevents bias. For example, it stops situations where Student A thinks: "If I give B a good rating, B will give me a good rating too." The system ensures that the pairs are one-directional.

**Step 3: Student fills the peer review form**
Each student sees a form about their assigned peer(s). The form contains questions about that peer's performance, behaviour, teamwork, participation, and other criteria. The exact questions come from the template – specifically, the fields that are marked as "peer-owned" in Template Management.

**What the peer review form might ask:**
- "How well does this student participate in group activities?" (Rating: Excellent, Good, Developing, Needs Improvement)
- "Does this student complete their share of the work in group projects?" (Yes/No)
- "How respectful is this student towards classmates?" (Rating)
- "Would you want to work with this student again?" (Yes/No)
- "Write one strength of this student:" (Text)
- "Write one suggestion for improvement:" (Text)

**Step 4: Responses are anonymous**
The student being reviewed NEVER sees who gave which rating. Peer responses are visible ONLY to the teacher. The teacher can see all the reviews but the students only see their own feedback (if the school chooses to share it).

**Why anonymity is important:**
- Students give honest feedback without fear of hurting someone's feelings.
- Students do not feel pressured to give good ratings to friends.
- The feedback is more truthful and useful for the teacher.

**Real-world example:**
> Class 8-A has 30 students. For Cycle 1, the teacher starts the peer assessment. The system automatically creates 15 pairs (each student reviews one peer). No self-reviews. No reciprocal pairs. Priya is asked to review Rahul. She opens the form and sees questions about Rahul's group work, behaviour, and participation. She answers honestly and submits. Rahul does NOT review Priya – he reviews a different student. Nobody knows who reviewed whom, except the teacher.

## Feature 5: Submission and Locking

When the student has finished filling all their sections, they click the **"Submit"** button at the bottom of the form.

**What happens after the student clicks Submit:**

1. **The card is locked for editing.** The student can no longer change any of their answers. All the text boxes, drop-down menus, and rating fields become read-only. The student can still open the card and look at their answers, but they cannot modify them.

2. **Status changes to "Completed".** On the student's dashboard, the card status changes from "In Progress" to "Completed". The completion percentage shows 100%.

3. **The teacher can see the student's work.** The teacher can now open the card and see everything the student wrote – the self-reflection, strengths, areas for improvement, self-ratings, goals (if applicable), and peer reviews.

4. **The teacher is notified.** The system notifies the teacher that the student has submitted their part. The teacher can now review it and incorporate it into the final card.

**Why lock the card after submission?**
- Once the student submits, the teacher can rely on that data being final. The teacher does NOT want the student changing their self-assessment after the teacher has already reviewed it.
- It prevents students from changing answers after seeing their grades or the teacher's comments.
- It creates a clear boundary: "The student's work is done. Now it is the teacher's turn."

**What if the student made a mistake after submitting?**
The student cannot fix it themselves. They need to ask the teacher to unlock or reset the section. The teacher has a button to "Reset Student Submission" which puts the card back to "In Progress" status. The student can then fix their mistake and submit again.

This is a deliberate design choice. It prevents students from changing answers after seeing their grades, but it also allows for genuine mistakes to be corrected by asking the teacher.

## Important Rules to Remember

1. **Only Middle and Secondary students use this portal.** Foundation and Preparatory students (Nursery to Class 5) do NOT have access. Their cards are filled by teachers and parents only.

2. **Students see only their own fields.** The system shows only the fields that are marked as "student-owned" in the template configuration. The student cannot see teacher comments, parent observations, or peer reviews about themselves.

3. **Peer reviews are anonymous.** The student being reviewed never knows who gave the rating. Only the teacher can see who reviewed whom.

4. **No self-review, no reciprocal pairs.** These rules prevent bias in peer assessment.

5. **Submission is final (unless the teacher resets).** Once the student clicks Submit, their answers are locked. They need the teacher's help to make changes.

6. **The Goals Wizard is only for Classes 11 and 12.** Younger students do not see the wizard.

7. **Students can save and continue later.** The student does not need to finish everything in one sitting. They can save their progress, log out, and come back later.

## Common Questions from Students

### I cannot see the Student Portal. What do I do?

The Student Portal is only available for students in Middle (Classes 6 to 10) and Secondary (Classes 11 and 12). If you are in a younger class, you will not see it. If you are in the right class but still cannot see it, your school may not have enabled this feature yet, or you may need a special permission from your school's system administrator. Talk to your class teacher.

### I filled my self-assessment and clicked Submit, but I made a mistake. Can I fix it?

You cannot fix it yourself because the card is locked after submission. You need to ask your teacher to "reset" your submission. The teacher has a button that can put your card back to "In Progress" status so you can edit it again. After you fix your mistake, you must submit again.

### Can my teacher see what I wrote in my self-assessment?

Yes. The teacher can see everything you write in your self-assessment, your strengths, your areas for improvement, your learning preferences, and your self-ratings. This is the whole point – the teacher wants to understand your perspective so they can help you better.

### Can my parents see my self-assessment?

No. The parent portal only shows parent-owned fields. Your self-assessment is NOT visible to your parents through the parent portal. However, the teacher may choose to share parts of your self-assessment with your parents during parent-teacher meetings.

### Can I see what other students wrote in their self-assessments?

No. You can only see your own self-assessment. Other students' self-assessments are private to them and their teachers.

### I was assigned to review a classmate. Can they see who reviewed them?

No. Peer reviews are completely anonymous. The student being reviewed never knows who gave which rating. Only the teacher can see who reviewed whom.

### What if I do not want to review my classmate?

Peer assessment is a required part of the Holistic Progress Card process. However, if you have a genuine concern (for example, you do not feel comfortable reviewing a particular classmate), talk to your teacher privately. The teacher can reassign your review to another student.

### Can I review myself?

No. The system does NOT allow self-review. You can never be asked to review your own work.

### What is the Goals Wizard? Do I have to fill it?

The Goals Wizard is only for students in Classes 11 and 12. It is a step-by-step form that helps you think about your future – your career, college plans, skills, and personal goals. It is not mandatory, but it is highly recommended. The information you provide can help your teachers and parents guide you better.

### I started the Goals Wizard but did not finish. Will I lose my progress?

No. The wizard has a "Save and Continue Later" button on every page. Click this button before you close the page. When you come back later, the wizard will take you back to the exact page where you stopped. Your answers are saved.

### Can I change my goals after saving them?

Yes. As long as you have NOT clicked the Submit button on your card, you can go back to the Goals Wizard and change your goals at any time. You can add new goals, edit existing ones, or delete goals you no longer want.

### What happens after I submit my card?

After you click Submit:
1. Your card status changes to "Completed".
2. You can no longer edit your answers.
3. Your teacher is notified that your part is done.
4. The teacher can now review your self-assessment, goals, and peer reviews.
5. Your contributions become part of your final Holistic Progress Card.

### How do I know if my teacher has seen my submission?

Your teacher will typically discuss your self-assessment with you during class or in a one-on-one meeting. If you want to know whether your teacher has reviewed your submission, you can ask them directly. The dashboard does NOT show a "Teacher has viewed" indicator.

## Common Questions for School Administrators

### Why are we building a Student Portal? Is it mandatory?

The Student Portal is part of the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020's "multi-contributor" approach to student assessment. The idea is that students should have a voice in their own assessment. It is not legally mandatory, but it is considered a best practice for holistic education. Many schools adopt it to provide a more complete picture of student development.

### Which classes should use the Student Portal?

NEP 2020 recommends self-assessment for Middle and Secondary stage students (Classes 6 to 12). Younger students (Classes 1 to 5) are not developmentally ready for self-assessment. However, your school can decide which classes to include. Talk to your Principal about what works best for your school.

### How many peer review cycles should we have?

The system supports 1 to 9 cycles per template. Most schools use 1 or 2 cycles per term. More cycles give more data but require more time. Start with 1 cycle and increase if needed.

### Can we add our own questions to the self-assessment form?

Yes. The self-assessment fields are defined in Template Management. An administrator can add, remove, or modify the fields that students see. The fields must be marked as "student-owned" in the template configuration.

### How do we ensure students take peer assessment seriously?

Teachers can:
- Explain the purpose and importance of peer assessment to students.
- Emphasise that reviews are anonymous.
- Monitor the quality of reviews and follow up with students who give obviously biased or careless reviews.
- Use the peer review data as part of the student's overall assessment (if the school chooses to).

## Current Status – What Is Built vs What Is Missing

**Important note for school administrators:** The Student Portal screens and logic are built, but there is a missing piece that prevents the system from actually working. Here is the current status:

| Part | Is It Built? | What This Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Screen designs (views)** | ✅ Yes – Built | The student dashboard, self-assessment form, goals wizard, and peer review screens all exist. You can navigate to them. They look complete. |
| **Behind-the-scenes logic (controllers and services)** | ✅ Yes – Built | The code that decides what to show, who can see what, how to pair students for peer review, and how to process submissions is all written and ready. |
| **Database tables** | ❌ No – NOT Built | The actual database tables that would store student submissions, self-assessment answers, goal entries, and peer review responses do NOT exist yet. The model files (blueprints) have been created, but the database migration (the step that actually creates the tables in the database) has NOT been run. |

**What this means in practice:**
- You CAN open the student portal screens and see the forms. They look exactly as described in this document.
- BUT if a student tries to **save** their self-assessment or submit a peer review, the system will fail. It will show an error because there is no database table to store the data in.

**Analogy:** The screens are like a car with a beautiful body, a steering wheel, seats, and a dashboard. Everything inside looks ready. But there is no engine under the hood. The car looks complete but it cannot move.

**What needs to happen:** A software developer needs to create and run the database migration. This is a technical step that actually creates the tables in the school's database. Once that migration is run, the Student Portal will work from start to finish – students will be able to save their work, submit their assessments, and teachers will be able to view the results.

**Estimated developer effort:** This is a small task for a developer who knows the system. It involves running one command to create the tables. The developer does NOT need to write new screens or logic – just enable the missing database piece.
