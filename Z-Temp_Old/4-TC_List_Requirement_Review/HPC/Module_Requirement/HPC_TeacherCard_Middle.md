# HPC Teacher Card — Middle Template (BV3)
**For grades:** 7th, 8th, 9th, 10th
**Total Pages:** 46 pages (tabs)

## What is this?

This is the main screen where teachers fill the Holistic Progress Card for students in Classes 7, 8, 9, and 10. This is the **largest template** with 46 pages (tabs). By this stage, students study many subjects, participate in many activities, and their assessment is more detailed. This card combines information from the teacher, the student themselves, their parents, and even their classmates.

Think of it like a big report file with 46 sections — each covering a different part of the student's growth. If you printed all 46 pages, it would be as thick as a small book.

### Why 46 Pages?

The Middle template is the biggest because middle school has the most subjects and assessment areas. Here is how the 46 pages are used:

- **Academic Subjects (about 22 pages):** Science splits into Physics, Chemistry, and Biology. Social Studies splits into History, Geography, and Civics. Mathematics covers Algebra, Geometry, and Statistics. Languages cover English, Hindi, and sometimes a third language. Each subject needs 2-4 pages.
- **Life Skills (about 4 pages):** Communication, teamwork, problem-solving, self-awareness, empathy, decision-making.
- **Co-Curricular Activities (about 5 pages):** Sports, art, music, dance, drama, debate, clubs.
- **Physical Education & Health (about 3 pages):** Fitness tests, yoga, sports skills, health awareness.
- **Values Education (about 3 pages):** Honesty, respect, civic sense, environmental awareness.
- **Vocational / Work Education (about 3 pages):** Computer skills, gardening, carpentry, etc.
- **Student Self-Assessment (about 2 pages):** The student rates themselves on the same skills.
- **Parent Feedback (about 1 page):** Parents' observations and comments.
- **Overall Summary (about 3 pages):** Teacher's overall remarks, principal's sign-off, attendance summary.

The exact number of pages for each subject depends on the school's curriculum and the template configuration set by the school admin.

## Page 1 — School & Student Information

This page is the same as in Foundation and Preparatory templates. Most fields fill themselves from the school's computer system. The teacher just checks the information.

### School Details (auto-filled)
- **School Name:** The full school name (e.g., "Delhi Public School"). Comes from the school's registration.
- **Address, Village, Town:** Location details from school records.
- **BRC, CRC:** Education office codes set by the government.
- **State:** The state where the school is located.
- **Pincode (6 boxes):** Type one digit per box.
- **UDISE Code (11 boxes):** Government school ID — one digit per box.
- **Medium of Instruction:** The teaching language (e.g., "English," "Hindi").
- **Rural/Urban:** Village or city classification.
- **Affiliation Number, Email, Website, Phone Numbers:** School contact info.

### Student Details (auto-filled from admission records)
- **Full Name:** First, middle, and last name. Comes from the admission form.
- **Admission Number:** The student's unique ID (e.g., "ADM-2021-0045").
- **Roll Number:** The student's roll number in class (e.g., "22").
- **Gender:** Boy or girl.
- **Date of Birth:** Shows as dd/mm/yyyy (e.g., "15/08/2012").
- **Class:** 7th, 8th, 9th, or 10th.
- **Section:** A, B, C, etc.
- **House:** Red, Blue, Green, Yellow, etc.
- **Aadhar ID:** 12-digit government ID. Twelve separate boxes.
- **APAAR ID:** Education ID. Separate boxes for each digit.
- **Birth Certificate Number:** From the birth certificate.
- **Student Photo:** Space for the student's picture. Click "Upload" to add one.

### Parent / Guardian Details (auto-filled)
- Father's name, mother's name, guardian's name
- Phone numbers (father, mother, guardian) — 10-digit numbers
- Occupations (e.g., "Engineer," "Teacher," "Business," "Farmer")
- Email addresses

### Contact & Demographics (auto-filled)
- Mobile number, email, home address, home pincode
- Religion, caste, nationality, mother tongue, blood group, EWS status

### Physical Measurements (auto-filled from health records)
- Height in centimeters (e.g., "152 cm")
- Weight in kilograms (e.g., "45 kg")

### Attendance Table (same as other templates)
- One row per month from April to March
- **Total Working Days:** How many days school was open that month. Teacher types the number.
- **Days Present:** How many days the student attended. Teacher types the number.
- **Attendance Percentage:** Calculated automatically by the computer. Formula: Present ÷ Working × 100.
- **Reason for Absence:** Text box. Teacher explains long absences (e.g., "Was hospitalized for 10 days").
- **Add/Remove Rows:** Click **+** to add a new row. Click **-** on any row to delete it. A popup asks "Are you sure?" Click Yes to confirm.

### ASC Rubric Tables — Detailed Explanation

The **ASC Rubric Table** is the most important assessment tool on these pages. It helps the teacher rate how well the student performs a specific skill. Think of it like a checklist where you mark the student's level for each skill.

#### What ASC Stands For:

| Level | Name | Meaning | Analogy |
|-------|------|---------|---------|
| **A** | **Stream (Beginning)** | The student is just starting. They need a lot of guidance. They can do the skill only with teacher help. | Like a baby learning to walk — they hold your hand and stumble often. |
| **B** | **Mountain (Developing)** | The student is making progress. They can do the skill sometimes but still make mistakes. They need occasional help. | Like a child who can walk but still falls sometimes and needs someone nearby. |
| **C** | **Sky (Achieved)** | The student can do the skill on their own, every time. They are confident and accurate. | Like an adult who walks easily without thinking about it. |
| **D** | **Peak (Advanced)** | The student has mastered the skill. They can teach others or apply it in new situations. | Like a professional athlete who not only walks but runs marathons. |

#### What the Table Looks Like:

It is a grid with:
- **Left column:** The skill or criteria being assessed (e.g., "Formulates a hypothesis").
- **Middle columns:** One column for each level (Stream, Mountain, Sky, Peak).
- **Each cell:** A small circle (radio button). You can click only one circle per row. When you click a circle, it fills with a dot. Clicking another circle in the same row moves the dot to the new circle.

#### Example — Science Lab Skills Rubric:

| Skill | Stream (Beginning) | Mountain (Developing) | Sky (Achieved) | Peak (Advanced) |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Follows lab safety rules | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| Uses microscope correctly | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| Records observations accurately | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| Draws conclusions from data | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |

#### How to Fill It:

1. Look at the first row. Read the skill name in the left column: "Follows lab safety rules."
2. Think about the student's ability for this skill.
3. Click the circle under the level that matches the student:
   - If the student always follows safety rules (wears goggles, handles chemicals carefully) without being reminded → click **Sky** or **Peak**.
   - If the student knows the rules but sometimes forgets (needs reminders) → click **Mountain**.
   - If the student does not know the safety rules and needs constant supervision → click **Stream**.
4. Move to the next row and repeat.
5. You can change any selection by clicking a different circle in the same row.

#### Example of a Completed Row

For "Uses microscope correctly":
- Student can set up the microscope, focus on a slide, and adjust magnification without any help → click **Sky**.
- Student can focus but needs help setting up the slide and adjusting light → click **Mountain**.
- Student cannot find anything under the microscope without the teacher setting it up completely → click **Stream**.
- Student can not only use the microscope perfectly but can also help classmates with theirs → click **Peak**.

#### Tips for Teachers

- Be honest and fair. It is normal for a student to be at "Stream" for some skills and "Sky" for others.
- The rubric shows you exactly where the student needs more support. Use it to plan your teaching.
- You can change your selection at any time if the student improves later in the term.
- Do not feel pressured to give all "Sky" ratings. Honest assessment helps the student more.

### Multi-Source Assessment Data

One of the biggest differences in the Middle template is that it can show assessment data from multiple people, not just the teacher. This gives a fuller picture of the student.

#### 1. Student Self-Assessment

- **What it is:** The student rates themselves using the same rubric or a simpler version of it. They answer questions like "How well do you think you understand this topic?" or "Rate your own participation in group work."
- **How it appears on the card:** The student's ratings show next to the teacher's ratings, often in a different color. For example, the teacher's selections might be in black and the student's in blue. There might be two columns side by side — one labeled "Teacher" and one labeled "Student."
- **Example:** For the skill "Works well in groups," the student might rate themselves "Sky" (I work great in groups!) but the teacher rates them "Mountain" (the student sometimes dominates the conversation). The teacher can see this difference on the card and discuss it with the student during a one-on-one meeting.
- **How it gets on the card:** The student fills their self-assessment form on the student portal (a separate website for students) before the teacher fills this card. The system pulls the data in automatically. The teacher does not type it.
- **Why it matters:** It helps students reflect on their own learning. It also helps teachers understand how the student sees themselves.

#### 2. Parent Input / Observation

- **What it is:** Parents can submit their own observations about the student's behavior at home, study habits, and interests.
- **How it appears on the card:** Parent notes show in a separate section with a label like "Parent Observation:" followed by the parent's name and what they wrote. The parent notes cannot be edited by the teacher — they are read-only.
- **Example:** A parent might write "Priya spends 2 hours daily on homework but gets distracted by her phone. She needs help managing her time." The teacher reads this and can discuss time management strategies with the student.
- **How it gets on the card:** Parents fill a separate parent portal form on the school website. The system pulls their responses into the card automatically.
- **Why it matters:** Parents see the student at home and can share information the teacher might not know about.

#### 3. Peer Review Results

- **What it is:** Classmates give feedback on each other's collaboration, participation, and teamwork during group activities.
- **How it appears on the card:** A summary of peer ratings. Example: "Average peer rating for teamwork: 4.2 out of 5" or "Peer feedback: Always contributes ideas, sometimes interrupts others."
- **Important:** Peer reviews are anonymous. The names of the classmates who gave the feedback are never shown. Only the average score or summarized comment appears.
- **How it gets on the card:** Students fill peer review forms in class (on paper or on computers). The teacher or the system calculates averages and displays them.
- **Why it matters:** Classmates see each other's behavior during group work and can provide useful feedback that the teacher might miss.

#### 4. Teacher Assessment (Your Part)

- **What it is:** The teacher's own ratings, grades, comments, and scores for each subject.
- **How it appears:** These are the main fields on each page. The teacher fills them directly by clicking radio buttons in rubric tables, typing in text boxes, selecting from dropdowns, and entering scores.
- **How it gets on the card:** The teacher types or selects everything directly. This is the part the teacher controls.

### Competency Mapping to NEP 2020 Learning Outcomes

#### What Is NEP 2020?

NEP 2020 stands for National Education Policy 2020. It is a set of rules and guidelines from the Government of India that says what students should learn and be able to do at each grade level. These are called **Learning Outcomes**.

#### What Is Competency Mapping?

Each subject page shows which NEP 2020 Learning Outcomes the assessment on that page is linked to. This means when the teacher fills the rubric or marks, they are also checking whether the student has achieved the national learning standards.

#### How It Looks on the Page

- A small section or badge on each assessment page says: **"Mapped Competencies:"** followed by a list of codes and short descriptions.
- Example on a Science page:
  > **Mapped Competencies:**
  > - SCI.9.M.01 — Applies scientific methods to investigate phenomena
  > - SCI.9.M.02 — Demonstrates understanding of cell structure and function
- The teacher does not type these codes — they come from the template configuration automatically.
- When you fill the rubric for "Uses microscope correctly," you are indirectly assessing competency SCI.9.M.01.

#### Why This Matters

- It helps schools prove they are following the national curriculum.
- The report card becomes aligned with government requirements.
- It helps track whether students across India are achieving the expected learning outcomes.
- It makes the Holistic Progress Card recognized and accepted everywhere.

### Co-Curricular and Life Skills Pages

The Middle template has dedicated pages for non-academic areas. These are just as important as academic subjects because they assess the whole child.

**Life Skills Pages (examples of what they assess):**
- **Communication Skills:** Speaking in front of class, expressing opinions clearly, listening to others.
- **Collaboration:** Working in groups, sharing responsibilities, helping peers.
- **Problem-Solving:** Finding solutions, thinking creatively, analyzing situations.
- **Self-Awareness:** Knowing personal strengths and weaknesses, setting goals.
- **Empathy:** Understanding others' feelings, showing kindness, helping those in need.
- **Decision Making:** Making good choices, thinking before acting, considering consequences.

Each life skill page has its own ASC rubric with criteria specific to that skill.

**Co-Curricular Pages (examples):**
- **Sports & Games:** Proficiency in chosen sport, fitness level, sportsmanship, team spirit.
- **Art:** Drawing, painting, sculpture, creativity.
- **Music:** Singing, playing an instrument, music theory.
- **Dance:** Different dance forms, rhythm, expression.
- **Drama & Theatre:** Acting, stage presence, dialogue delivery.
- **Clubs:** Debate club, gardening club, robotics club, literary club.

**Physical Education Pages:**
- Fitness tests (running time in seconds, flexibility measurement, strength exercises)
- Yoga proficiency (asanas, breathing exercises)
- Sports skills assessment (batting, bowling, dribbling, etc.)
- Health awareness (knowledge of nutrition, hygiene, first aid)

**Values Education Pages:**
- Honesty and integrity (telling the truth, not cheating)
- Respect for others (teachers, elders, classmates)
- Civic sense (following rules, keeping surroundings clean, queue discipline)
- Environmental awareness (saving water, planting trees, reducing waste)
- National identity and pride (respecting the flag, national anthem)

### LMS Data Integration

If the school uses a Learning Management System (LMS) like Google Classroom or a school app, exam scores, quiz results, and assignment grades from the LMS appear automatically on the card. They appear in grey boxes that the teacher cannot edit. This saves the teacher time because they do not need to type test scores manually.

## Key Features

### Save Button
- At the bottom of the page. Saves ALL 46 pages at once.
- Click Save. A message says "Saved successfully." Required empty fields turn red and stop the save.
- **Always click Save before closing.** If you close without saving, all work on all 46 pages is lost.

### Auto-Fill
- Student info, school info, attendance percentages, LMS exam scores, peer review averages, parent observations, and student self-assessments all fill automatically.
- The teacher mainly fills rubric ratings, comments, and grades.

### Drag and Drop Reordering
- Each section has a drag handle (⠿ icon) on the left side.
- Click and hold the icon, move up or down, then release.
- This reorders sections within a page.

### Editable Labels
- Double-click any field label to rename it.
- Type the new name and press Enter.
- Schools can customize field names without programming.

### PDF Preview
- "Preview PDF" button shows a print-ready version of all 46 pages.
- Shows school logo, student details, and all assessment data.

## Example Scenario — How a Teacher Uses the Middle Card

**Example:** Mrs. Singh teaches Science to Class 8. She needs to fill the Holistic Progress Card for a student named Priya Sharma.

Mrs. Singh logs into the school system and opens Priya's card. She sees 46 tabs at the top of the screen.

**Page 1:** She checks that Priya's name, class (8th), section (A), and roll number (15) are correct. She scrolls to the attendance table. Priya has 95% attendance overall — excellent. No reason needed for absences.

**Page 2:** She clicks through to Page 2. Priya has already filled her profile in the student portal. Her dream job shows as "Doctor." Friends list shows "Neha" and "Aarav." Mrs. Singh does not need to change anything.

**Pages 3-6 (English):** Mrs. Singh reads the English rubric. For "Reading comprehension," Priya can analyze complex texts and answer inferential questions. Mrs. Singh clicks "Sky." For "Essay writing," Priya writes well but needs to work on structure. Mrs. Singh clicks "Mountain" and types a comment: "Excellent vocabulary. Work on organizing paragraphs with topic sentences."

**Pages 10-12 (Physics):** Mrs. Singh sees the multi-source assessment section. Priya's self-assessment shows she rated herself "Sky" for "Understanding Newton's laws." Mrs. Singh agrees — clicks "Sky" in the teacher column. For "Solving numerical problems," Priya rated herself "Sky" but Mrs. Singh knows Priya struggled on the last test. Mrs. Singh clicks "Mountain" and notes the difference. She makes a mental note to discuss this with Priya.

**Pages 13-15 (Chemistry):** The LMS has already filled Priya's test score: 42/50. Mrs. Singh does not need to type it. She fills the rubric for lab safety and equipment handling.

**Pages 31-33 (Life Skills):** Mrs. Singh reads the peer review section. Priya's classmates gave her 4.5/5 for teamwork. One comment says "Priya always helps others understand difficult topics." Mrs. Singh smiles — this matches her own observation. She clicks "Sky" for collaboration.

**After all 46 pages:** Mrs. Singh scrolls to the bottom and clicks Save. "Saved successfully!" appears. She closes the card.
