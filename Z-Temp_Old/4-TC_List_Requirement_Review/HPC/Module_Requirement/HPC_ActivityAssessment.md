# HPC Activity Assessment - Full Guide

## What Is This Feature?

Think of this as a dashboard. A dashboard is like the control panel of a car. It shows you everything at once without having to open doors or look in different places. This dashboard shows you the progress of every person who needs to fill in a student's progress card.

When a teacher makes a progress card for a student, many different people need to put information into that card. The teacher puts in grades and comments. The student might need to write about themselves. The parents might need to fill in a form. The student's classmates might need to rate the student.

Without this dashboard, the teacher would have to open each section of the card one by one to check if it is done. That takes a lot of time. The dashboard shows everything on one single screen. The teacher can see in two seconds who has finished and who is still working.

---

## Who Is Involved in Making a Card?

There are four different types of people who might need to put information into a student's progress card. Not all four are used for every student. Some cards only use two or three.

### Person 1: The Teacher

The teacher is the main person who fills in the card. The teacher writes grades for each subject. The teacher writes comments about the student's behavior and progress. The teacher fills in many pages of the card. This is the most important contribution.

Every card has teacher input. There is no card without the teacher.

The teacher fills in assessment pages. These pages have ratings, scores, and written comments. The teacher might need to fill 15 to 20 pages depending on the grade level.

### Person 2: The Student

For middle school and secondary school students, the student also writes about themselves. This is called self-assessment. The student answers questions like:

What am I good at?
What do I want to improve?
How do I feel about my progress?

The student fills in these answers on the computer or on paper. Then someone types them into the system.

Younger students (Foundation and Preparatory) do NOT do self-assessment. They are too young. Only middle and secondary students do this.

### Person 3: The Parent

The parent fills in an observation form. This is a form that asks the parent questions about the child's behavior at home. For example:

Does the child complete homework on time?
How does the child behave with family members?
What activities does the child enjoy at home?

The parent receives a link (like a web address) through email or WhatsApp. The parent clicks the link and fills in the form on their phone or computer.

Parents of all grade levels can fill in this form. But it is optional. Some parents do not fill it in.

### Person 4: Peers (Classmates)

For middle and secondary school students, classmates also give feedback. This is called peer evaluation. Students rate each other on things like:

Does this student work well in a group?
Does this student help others?
Is this student respectful?

Each student might rate three to five classmates. The teacher assigns who rates whom.

Younger students do NOT do peer evaluation. They are too young.

## What the Dashboard Looks Like

When the teacher opens the dashboard for a student, they see a screen that looks something like this example. This example is for a student named Priya Sharma in Class 5-A for Term 1.

At the top of the screen is the student's name and class:
Student: Priya Sharma | Class: 5-A | Term: 1

Below that is a section called CONTRIBUTOR STATUS. This shows each person or group involved in the card.

### Contributor Status Section

The contributor status section has one row for each contributor. Here is an example:

Teacher: Complete (18 out of 18 pages done)

This means the teacher has filled in all 18 pages of the card. There is no work left for the teacher to do on this card. The status shows a green checkmark and the word "Complete."

Student: In Progress (self-assessment started but not finished)

This means the student opened the self-assessment form and typed some answers. But the student did not click the Submit button yet. Some answers are filled in, but some are still empty. The status shows a yellow clock and the words "In Progress."

Parent: Pending (link sent, parent has not opened it yet)

This means the teacher sent the parent a link to fill in the observation form. But the parent has not clicked the link yet. No work has been done. The status shows a grey empty box and the word "Pending."

Peer: Complete (3 out of 3 peer reviews done)

This means all three classmates who were assigned to rate this student have finished their ratings. No more peer reviews are needed. The status shows a green checkmark and the word "Complete."

### Domain Progress Section

Below the contributor status, the dashboard shows a section called DOMAIN PROGRESS. A domain is a subject area or a skill area. For example, "Language and Literacy" is one domain. "Mathematics" is another domain.

For each domain, the teacher sees:

The domain name.
A colored bar showing how much of this domain is complete.
A percentage number next to the bar.

Here is an example of what the domain progress section might look like:

Language and Literacy: 100 percent complete. Green bar is full.

This means all assessment pages for Language and Literacy are completely filled in. The teacher has entered all the grades. The student has submitted their self-assessment. Everything for this domain is done.

Mathematics: 100 percent complete. Green bar is full.

Same as above. Everything for Mathematics is done.

Science: 80 percent complete. Yellow bar is mostly full but has some empty space.

This means some parts of the Science assessment are done, but not all. Maybe the teacher has filled in the grades, but the student has not submitted their self-assessment for Science. The bar shows 80 percent to indicate it is almost done.

Social Studies: 0 percent complete. Grey bar is completely empty.

This means no work has been started for Social Studies. The teacher has not entered any grades yet. Nothing has been done.

Life Skills: 100 percent complete. Green bar is full.

Everything for Life Skills is done.

## The Four Status Indicators

Every contributor and every domain has a status. There are four possible statuses. Each status has a specific color and a specific icon. The teacher can tell the status just by looking at the color, without even reading the words.

### Status 1: Complete (Green Checkmark)

The icon is a green checkmark. The color is green. The label says "Complete."

This means all required work for this contributor or domain is finished. There is nothing more to do. The teacher can move on to the next thing.

For the teacher contributor, "Complete" means all 18 pages have been filled in. For a domain, "Complete" means all assessment items in that domain have been filled in.

When the teacher sees green, they know that part is done.

### Status 2: In Progress (Yellow Clock)

The icon is a yellow clock. The color is yellow or orange. The label says "In Progress."

This means some work has been done, but not all. The person started the work but did not finish it. Or some parts of a domain are done but other parts are not.

The teacher sees yellow and knows: "This is not finished yet. I need to wait or remind someone."

### Status 3: Pending (Grey Empty Box)

The icon is a grey empty box. The color is grey. The label says "Pending."

This means no work has been done at all. The person has not even started. For a domain, none of the assessment items have been filled in.

The teacher sees grey and knows: "No one has touched this yet. I need to take action."

### Status 4: Not Applicable (Grey Cross)

The icon is a grey X mark. The color is grey. The label says "Not Applicable."

This means this contributor or domain does not apply to this student. For example, if the student is in Foundation stage (very young), peer evaluation and self-assessment do not apply. They show "Not Applicable."

This is not a problem. It is just how the system is designed. Some things are only for older students.

## A Complete Example: Mrs. Sharma Checks Aarav's Card

Let us walk through a real example so you can see how the dashboard works in practice.

### Situation

Mrs. Sharma is the class teacher of Class 5-A. She has been working on Aarav's progress card for two weeks. She has filled in most of the teacher pages. She wants to check if the card is ready to be finalized and sent to the principal for approval.

### Step 1: Mrs. Sharma Opens the Dashboard

Mrs. Sharma logs in to the system. She finds Aarav's name in her student list. She clicks on "Activity Assessment" next to Aarav's name. The dashboard opens.

### Step 2: Mrs. Sharma Looks at the Contributor Status

Mrs. Sharma looks at the top section of the dashboard. This is what she sees:

Teacher: In Progress (Yellow Clock)

Mrs. Sharma knows she still has 2 more pages to fill in for the Life Skills domain. She has finished everything else. The yellow clock reminds her that she needs to finish those two pages.

Student: Pending (Grey Empty Box)

Mrs. Sharma sees that Aarav has not even started his self-assessment. She needs to remind him in class tomorrow. She makes a mental note.

Parent: Pending (Grey Empty Box)

Mrs. Sharma sent the parent link to Aarav's mother three days ago. The mother has not opened it yet. Mrs. Sharma decides to send a reminder message on WhatsApp.

Peer: Complete (Green Checkmark)

The three classmates who were assigned to rate Aarav have all finished their reviews. This part is done.

### Step 3: Mrs. Sharma Looks at the Domain Progress

Now Mrs. Sharma looks at the domain progress section. This is what she sees:

Language and Literacy: Complete (Green, 100 percent)

Mrs. Sharma finished this domain last week. All grades are entered. No issues.

Mathematics: Complete (Green, 100 percent)

Also finished.

Science: In Progress (Yellow, 80 percent)

Mrs. Sharma realizes she entered grades for Science but did not write the teacher comments yet. The 80 percent tells her that most of the work is done, but not all.

Social Studies: Pending (Grey, 0 percent)

Mrs. Sharma has not touched Social Studies at all. She has been busy with other subjects. She needs to start this soon.

Life Skills: In Progress (Yellow, 60 percent)

Mrs. Sharma started Life Skills but did not finish. She needs to complete those last two pages.

### Step 4: Mrs. Sharma Plans Her Actions

Based on what she sees on the dashboard, Mrs. Sharma makes a plan:

1. Tonight: Finish the two remaining Life Skills pages.
2. Tomorrow: Remind Aarav in class to start his self-assessment.
3. Tomorrow: Send another WhatsApp message to Aarav's mother.
4. This week: Complete Science teacher comments and start Social Studies.

Without the dashboard, Mrs. Sharma would have to open each page of the card one by one to find out what is missing. With the dashboard, she knows everything in 30 seconds.

## When Different People Use This Dashboard

The dashboard is useful for different people at different times.

### When the Teacher Uses It

The teacher uses the dashboard before finalizing the card. The teacher wants to make sure everything is complete before sending the card for approval.

The teacher checks:

Have I filled in all my pages?
Has the student submitted their self-assessment?
Has the parent submitted their observation form?
Have the peer reviews been done?

If anything is not complete, the teacher takes action. The teacher might:

Finish their own remaining pages.
Remind the student to do self-assessment.
Send a new link to the parent.
Assign more peer reviewers.

### When the Principal Uses It

The principal can see a dashboard for any student in the school. The principal uses this to check if cards are ready for approval.

The principal might look at a class and see that 20 out of 30 students have complete cards. The remaining 10 students are still waiting for parent input or student self-assessment. The principal can tell the class teacher to follow up with those parents and students.

The principal can also see which teachers are behind schedule. If one teacher has many incomplete cards, the principal can offer help or ask what is wrong.

### When the Admin Uses It

The school admin can see dashboards across all classes. The admin can generate reports like:

Completion rate for Class 5: 80 percent.
Completion rate for Class 6: 60 percent.
Average completion time per card: 3 weeks.

The admin uses this information to plan when to print cards and when to schedule parent-teacher meetings.

## The Journey of a Card

Every card goes through a journey from start to finish. The dashboard helps at every stage of this journey.

### Stage 1: Draft

The teacher starts filling in the card. The teacher has not finished yet. The dashboard shows the teacher their own progress. It helps the teacher track how much work remains.

At this stage, only the teacher's status matters. The student and parent sections are not relevant yet because the teacher is still working.

### Stage 2: Waiting for Student

The teacher has finished all their sections. The card is ready for student self-assessment. The dashboard now shows:

Teacher: Complete (Green)
Student: Pending (Grey)

The teacher looks at the dashboard every day. When the student finally submits, the status changes to:

Student: Complete (Green)

The teacher knows it is time to move to the next stage.

### Stage 3: Waiting for Parent

After the student submits, the teacher sends the parent link. The dashboard shows:

Teacher: Complete (Green)
Student: Complete (Green)
Parent: Pending (Grey)

The teacher waits for the parent to fill in the observation form. After a few days, the teacher checks the dashboard again. If the parent still shows Pending, the teacher sends a reminder.

When the parent submits, the status changes to:

Parent: Complete (Green)

### Stage 4: Ready for Review

Now all contributors show Complete. The dashboard shows:

Teacher: Complete (Green)
Student: Complete (Green)
Parent: Complete (Green)
Peer: Complete (Green)

The teacher knows the card is ready to be submitted for the principal's review. The teacher clicks the "Submit for Review" button. The principal receives a notification.

### Stage 5: Approval

The principal opens the card, reviews everything, and either approves or sends it back with comments.

If the principal approves, the card is published. The card can now be printed or emailed to parents.

If the principal sends it back, the teacher needs to make changes and resubmit.

## What Is Currently Working and What Is Not

This dashboard was built in stages. Some parts work perfectly. Some parts do not work yet because the database tables they need have not been created yet.

### What Works: The Dashboard Screen

The dashboard screen itself exists. When a teacher opens it, the screen displays correctly. The layout is correct. The colors and icons are correct. The screen does not crash.

### What Works: The Behind-the-Scenes Code

The code that gathers data and prepares the dashboard is written. This code runs in the background when the dashboard opens. It collects information from different parts of the system.

### What Works: Teacher Data

The teacher's completion percentage is accurate. This works because the teacher's data is stored in database tables that already exist. The system can count how many pages the teacher has filled in and compare that to how many pages need to be filled in.

When the dashboard shows "Teacher: 80 percent complete," that number is correct. The teacher can trust it.

### What Is Partially Working: Student Data

The dashboard tries to show whether the student has submitted their self-assessment. But this depends on a database table that stores student submissions. That database table does NOT exist yet.

Because the table does not exist, the dashboard might show wrong information. It might show "Pending" even when the student has submitted. Or it might show an error. The student data is not reliable right now.

This will be fixed when a developer creates the student submission database table.

### What Is Partially Working: Parent Data

The dashboard tries to show whether the parent has submitted their observation form. But this depends on a database table that stores parent submissions. That database table does NOT exist yet.

Because the table does not exist, the parent data might show "Pending" even when the parent has submitted. Or it might not show anything at all. The parent data is not reliable right now.

This will be fixed when a developer creates the parent submission database table.

### What Is Partially Working: Peer Data

The dashboard tries to show how many peer reviews have been completed. But this depends on a database table that stores peer assessments. That database table does NOT exist yet.

Because the table does not exist, the peer data might show "0 out of 3" even when classmates have finished their reviews. The peer data is not reliable right now.

This will be fixed when a developer creates the peer assessment database table.

### Summary of What Needs to Be Built

A developer needs to create three new database tables:

1. A table for student self-assessment submissions. This table will store whether each student has submitted their self-assessment and what answers they gave.

2. A table for parent observation submissions. This table will store whether each parent has submitted their observation form and what answers they gave.

3. A table for peer assessments. This table will store which classmates rated which student, and what ratings they gave.

Once all three tables are created and connected to the dashboard, the dashboard will show accurate information for all contributors. Until then, only the teacher's data is reliable.

## Technical Notes for Developers

This section is for people who build or maintain the software. Regular users can skip this part.

### How the Dashboard Calculates Completion

The dashboard calculates completion by counting filled fields versus total required fields.

For the teacher: The system counts how many assessment fields the teacher has filled in across all pages. It divides this by the total number of required fields. This gives a percentage.

For the student: The system checks if a row exists in the student submissions table for this student and this term. If a row exists and the submission status is "submitted," the student is marked Complete.

For the parent: The system checks if a row exists in the parent submissions table for this student's parent token. If a row exists and the submission status is "submitted," the parent is marked Complete.

For peers: The system counts how many peer review records exist for this student and divides by the expected number of peer reviews.

### Database Migrations Needed

The following database migrations need to be created:

1. `create_student_hpc_submissions_table` - Stores student self-assessment data
2. `create_parent_hpc_observation_table` - Stores parent observation data
3. `create_peer_hpc_assessments_table` - Stores peer review data

Each migration should include the necessary foreign key relationships to link submissions to students, terms, and templates.

### Error Handling

If any of the missing tables do not exist, the dashboard should show a fallback message instead of crashing. The fallback message could be: "Data for this section is not yet available."

The current code attempts to read from these tables and might throw errors if the tables do not exist. The code should be updated to check for table existence before querying.

## Important Rules to Remember

1. The dashboard is a view-only screen. You cannot edit anything from the dashboard. You can only see the status.

2. The teacher's data is always accurate. Student, parent, and peer data might be wrong until the missing database tables are created.

3. The dashboard does not automatically refresh. You may need to close it and open it again to see updated status.

4. "Not Applicable" is not an error. It just means that contributor type is not relevant for this student's grade level.

5. The dashboard helps you track progress but does not automatically send reminders. You still need to remind students and parents manually.

## Summary

The Activity Assessment dashboard is a control panel that shows the progress of everyone involved in filling a student's card. It shows four contributors: teacher, student, parent, and peers. Each contributor has a status: Complete (green), In Progress (yellow), Pending (grey), or Not Applicable (grey X).

The dashboard saves time by showing everything on one screen. Without it, the teacher would have to open each page individually to check what is done.

The dashboard currently shows teacher data correctly. Student, parent, and peer data are not fully working because the required database tables have not been created yet. Once those tables are added, the dashboard will show complete and accurate information for all contributors.
