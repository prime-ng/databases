# HPC Teacher Card — Preparatory Template (BV2)
**For grades:** 4th, 5th, 6th
**Total Pages:** 30 pages (tabs)

## What is this?

This is the main screen where the class teacher fills the Holistic Progress Card for students in Classes 4, 5, and 6. It is very similar to the Foundation template (for younger kids) but has more pages — 30 tabs instead of 18. The extra pages are for more school subjects and more detailed assessment. Think of it like a big digital folder with 30 sections inside, one for each topic.

At this level, students start taking more formal tests, so the card includes space for marks, scores, and grades. It also connects with the school's Learning Management System (LMS) — a computer system that tracks homework, quizzes, and exams. If the school uses an LMS, some scores appear automatically on the card. The teacher does not need to type them.

The screen shows one page at a time. At the top, there are 30 small tabs in a row. The tab you are working on is highlighted. Click any tab to go to that page.

## Page 1 — School & Student Information

This page is almost identical to the Foundation template's Page 1. All the basic information about the school and the student fills itself automatically from the school's computer system. The teacher just checks if everything is correct and makes changes only if something is wrong.

### What you see on this page:

**School Details (auto-filled — you just check them):**
- **School Name:** The full name of the school (e.g., "Delhi Public School"). Comes from the school's registration data.
- **Address:** The full street address of the school.
- **Village / Town:** Which village or town the school is in.
- **BRC:** Block Resource Centre — a local government education office code.
- **CRC:** Cluster Resource Centre — a smaller education cluster code.
- **State:** Which state (e.g., "Uttar Pradesh," "Karnataka").
- **Pincode:** 6 separate boxes for the postal code. Type one digit per box.
- **UDISE Code:** 11 separate boxes for the government school ID number.
- **Medium of Instruction:** The teaching language (e.g., "Hindi," "English," "Marathi").
- **Rural / Urban:** Whether the school is in a village or city.
- **Affiliation Number:** School's board number (CBSE, ICSE, etc.).
- **Email:** School's email address.
- **Website:** School's website address.
- **Phone Numbers:** School's contact number(s).

**Student Details (auto-filled from admission records):**
- **Full Name:** First name, middle name, last name. Comes from the admission form that parents filled when the student joined the school.
- **Admission Number:** The student's unique ID from the school (e.g., "ADM-2022-0156").
- **Roll Number:** The student's roll number in the class (e.g., "15").
- **Gender:** Boy or girl.
- **Date of Birth:** The student's birth date. Shows as dd/mm/yyyy (e.g., "12/05/2014").
- **Class:** Which class (4th, 5th, or 6th).
- **Section:** Which section (A, B, C, etc.).
- **House:** Which school house (Red, Blue, Green, Yellow).
- **Aadhar ID:** 12-digit government ID. Shown as 12 boxes, one digit each.
- **APAAR ID:** Another government education ID. Also digit-by-digit boxes.
- **Birth Certificate Number:** The number from the student's birth certificate.
- **Student Photo:** A space for the student's picture. Either already uploaded or you can click "Upload" to add one.

**Parent / Guardian Details (auto-filled):**
- Father's name, mother's name, guardian's name
- Phone numbers for father, mother, and guardian (10-digit mobile numbers)
- Occupations (e.g., "Teacher," "Farmer," "Business," "Government Employee")
- Email addresses for father, mother, and guardian

**Contact Information (auto-filled):**
- Family mobile number
- Family email address
- Home address
- Home pincode (6 boxes)

**Physical Measurements (auto-filled from health records):**
- Height in centimeters (e.g., "135 cm")
- Weight in kilograms (e.g., "30 kg")
These come from the school's health checkup records.

**Demographics (auto-filled):**
- Religion (e.g., "Hindu," "Muslim," "Sikh," "Christian")
- Caste (e.g., "General," "SC," "ST," "OBC")
- Nationality (e.g., "Indian")
- Mother Tongue (e.g., "Hindi," "Bengali," "Tamil")
- Blood Group (e.g., "A+," "B-," "O+")
- EWS Status (Yes/No — Economically Weaker Section)

### Attendance Table — Same as Foundation

A table with one row per month (April to March). The table has 5 columns:

| Month | Total Working Days | Days Present | Attendance % | Reason for Absence |

- **Month:** Each month gets one row. April is the first row, March is the last.
- **Total Working Days:** How many days school was open that month. Does not include Sundays, holidays, and breaks. Type the number (e.g., "22" for a month with 30 days minus 8 non-school days).
- **Days Present:** How many days the student came to school. Type the number (e.g., "20" if the student was absent 2 days).
- **Attendance Percentage:** Calculated automatically. Formula: Days Present ÷ Total Working Days × 100. For 20 out of 22, the computer shows "90.9%." You do not type this.
- **Reason for Absence:** A text box where the teacher can type why the student was absent for a long period (e.g., "Had chickenpox for 2 weeks," "Family went out of town for a wedding"). You can leave this blank for short absences.

**Example row for April:**
- Total Working Days: 22
- Days Present: 20
- Attendance %: 90.9 (auto-calculated)
- Reason for Absence: "Had fever for 3 days"

**How to add/remove rows:**
- Click the **+** (plus) button at the bottom of the table to add a new row for a special period or extra month.
- Click the **-** (minus) button on any row to delete it. A popup asks "Are you sure?" Click Yes to delete, No to keep.

- **Birthday:** A calendar date picker. Click the box. A small calendar pops up showing the current month. Click the left or right arrows to change months. Click the student's birth date (e.g., "12"). The date shows as "12/05/2014."

## Pages 3 to 30 — Subject / Domain & Assessment Pages (28 Pages)

### How These Pages Work

Pages 3 through 30 are **dynamically generated**. This means the system reads a "template recipe" stored in the database and builds each page automatically. The template recipe says: "For this grade level, show these subjects, and in each subject, show these fields." The school admin or curriculum coordinator sets up the recipe once. The teacher just fills the fields.

Think of it like a pre-printed form. The form already has the questions printed on it. The teacher just writes the answers.

### What Subjects Appear

Because this is for older students (Classes 4-6), there are more school subjects than in Foundation. The template decides which subjects appear. Typical subjects include:

- **Language Skills:** English, Hindi, or regional language — includes reading, writing, speaking, and grammar. Example fields: "Reading fluency grade," "Spelling test score," "Grammar assessment."
- **Mathematics:** Numbers, shapes, word problems, tables, measurement. Example fields: "Score on multiplication test," "Can solve word problems (Yes/No)," "Overall Math grade."
- **Science Understanding:** Plants, animals, weather, food, the human body. Example fields: "Science project score," "Lab observation notes," "Understanding of concepts grade."
- **Social Science:** Community, maps, history, culture. Example fields: "Map reading ability," "History project grade," "Class participation."
- **Art & Craft:** Drawing, painting, clay work, craft projects. Example fields: "Upload artwork," "Creativity grade," "Effort level."
- **Physical Education:** Sports, games, yoga, fitness. Example fields: "Fitness test results," "Sports skill level," "Teamwork rating."
- **Music & Dance:** Singing, instruments, dance movements. Example fields: "Music performance grade," "Rhythm assessment."
- **Life Skills:** Teamwork, sharing, helping others, making decisions. Example fields: "Works well with others (Yes/No)," "Problem-solving ability."
- **Work Education:** Simple tasks like gardening, cleaning, organizing. Example fields: "Task completion grade," "Participation level."

Each subject can have one or more pages. For example, "Language Skills" might have one page for Reading and another for Writing.

### What Each Page Contains

Each page follows the same pattern:

**1. Page Title:** At the top in bold letters. Example: "Language Skills — Reading" or "Mathematics — Geometry."

**2. Sections:** Each page has sections. A section is a group of related fields with its own heading. Sections have a colored background or a line separating them. For example, the "Mathematics" page might have sections called "Numbers," "Addition & Subtraction," and "Multiplication & Division."

**3. Three-Column Layout:** Fields are arranged in 3 columns side by side across the screen. This saves space and reduces scrolling. You fill fields from left to right, top to bottom.

### Types of Assessment Fields on These Pages

Here is every kind of field you might see, with step-by-step instructions:

**1. Text Input (Short Answer)**
- **What it is:** A single empty line where you type words.
- **Example label:** "Teacher's Comment:" — the teacher types "Rahul reads fluently but needs to work on comprehension."
- **How to fill:** Click inside the box with your mouse. A blinking line (cursor) appears. Start typing. To delete a mistake, press the Backspace key. Press the Space key to add a space between words.
- **Real example:** Label: "Observations about student's participation in group activities." Teacher types: "Rahul participates actively and helps his group members understand difficult concepts."

**2. Numeric Input (Number)**
- **What it is:** A box that accepts only numbers. If you try to type a letter, nothing shows up.
- **Example label:** "Score out of 50:" — the teacher types "42."
- **How to fill:** Click inside the box. Type digits 0-9 using the number keys at the top of the keyboard or the number pad on the right.
- **Real example:** Label: "Spelling test score (out of 20)." Teacher types: "18."

**3. Grade Dropdown**
- **What it is:** A menu with grade levels. Click the arrow to see the list.
- **Options might be:** A (Excellent), B (Good), C (Satisfactory), D (Needs Improvement), E (Not Assessed) — or: Beginning, Developing, Proficient, Advanced.
- **Example label:** "Overall Reading Grade:" — teacher picks "B" (Good) from the list.
- **How to fill:** Click the dropdown arrow. A list appears below. Move your mouse over the options. Click the one you want. The list closes and your choice shows in the box.

**4. Yes / No Toggle**
- **What it is:** Two small circles or a switch — one says "Yes," one says "No." You click one to select it. The selected option gets highlighted or filled.
- **Example label:** "Can multiply 2-digit numbers:" — click "Yes" if the student can, "No" if they cannot.
- **How to fill:** Click the word "Yes" or the circle next to it. It becomes colored or filled. The other option becomes grey.

**5. Checkbox (Multiple Choices)**
- **What it is:** Small square boxes. You can put a checkmark in more than one box.
- **Example label:** "Skills Demonstrated:" 
  ☐ Addition
  ☐ Subtraction
  ☐ Multiplication
  ☐ Division
  Check all that apply.
- **How to fill:** Click each box you want to check. A checkmark (✓) appears inside. Click again to remove the checkmark.
- **Real example:** Label: "Areas needing improvement:" with boxes for ☐ Reading fluency, ☐ Spelling, ☐ Handwriting, ☐ Math facts, ☐ Attention. Teacher checks the ones the student needs to work on.

**6. Image Upload**
- **What it is:** An "Upload" button that opens your computer files to select a picture.
- **Example label:** "Upload student's artwork" — the teacher takes a photo of the student's drawing or painting and uploads it.
- **How to fill:**
  1. Click the "Upload" or "Choose File" button.
  2. A window opens showing the files and folders on your computer.
  3. Navigate to the folder where the picture is saved.
  4. Click the picture file (it usually has a name ending in .jpg or .png).
  5. Click the "Open" button.
  6. The window closes. The picture appears on the form as a small image.
  7. If the wrong picture was uploaded, click "Remove" and start again.

**7. File Upload**
- **What it is:** Same as image upload but for any document file (PDF, Word document, etc.).
- **Example label:** "Upload project report" — the teacher uploads the student's project file saved as a PDF.
- **How to fill:** Same steps as image upload. Click "Upload," find the file, click "Open."

**8. JSON Structured Data**
- **What it is:** A behind-the-scenes way to store data. You usually see simple dropdowns or text boxes on screen. The system stores the data in a special format (JSON) that computers can read easily.
- **Example:** "Competency Mapping" — pick a competency from one dropdown (e.g., "Reading Comprehension") and a level from another dropdown (e.g., "Developing"). The system saves this as structured data behind the scenes.
- **Important:** You do not need to know what JSON is or how it works. Just fill the fields on the screen normally.

## Learning Data from LMS (Learning Management System)

### What is LMS Data?

LMS stands for Learning Management System. This is a separate computer system that the school may use for managing homework, quizzes, exams, and assignments. Examples include Google Classroom, Moodle, or a school-specific app.

If the school uses an LMS, the HPC Teacher Card can connect to it and pull in student learning data automatically. Think of it like two computers talking to each other — the LMS has the test scores, and the card reads them so the teacher does not need to type them again.

### What Data Gets Pulled In?

The following types of data can come from the LMS:

- **Exam Scores:** Results from unit tests, mid-terms, and final exams. Example: "Ravi scored 78 out of 100 in Mathematics."
- **Quiz Results:** Scores from online quizzes the student took on the computer. Example: "Science Quiz 3: 9/10 correct."
- **Homework Completion:** Whether the student submitted homework and the marks they got. Example: "Homework 5: Submitted (Grade: B)."
- **Assignment Grades:** Grades for projects, worksheets, and assignments. Example: "Science Project: A."
- **Activity Participation:** How often the student participated in online discussions or activities. Example: "Class discussions: 12 posts."

### How It Appears on the Page

- These pre-filled scores appear in fields with a grey background. The grey color means "read-only" — you can see the data but you cannot change it.
- If the teacher needs to correct a score (e.g., because of a grading error), they must contact the LMS administrator. They cannot edit it on this card.
- If there is no LMS, or if the LMS is not connected, these fields stay empty (white background). The teacher types the scores manually in that case.

### Why This Matters for Preparatory Level

At the Preparatory level (Classes 4-6), students start taking more formal tests and getting marks. The LMS data helps save the teacher time by filling in test scores automatically. Without the LMS, the teacher would need to look up test scores from a grade book and type them into every student's card. With the LMS, the scores appear automatically.

### Example of LMS Integration

- Ravi takes a Math test and scores 78 out of 100. The teacher enters the score in the LMS (or the LMS calculates it automatically). When the teacher opens Ravi's Teacher Card, the Mathematics section shows "78/100" in a grey box. The teacher can see it but cannot change it.
- Ravi submits his Science homework through the LMS. He gets a grade of B+. The B+ appears in the Science section of the card automatically.

## ASC Rubric Tables (For Rating Performance)

### What is an ASC Rubric Table?

A rubric is a table that helps the teacher rate the student's skill level in a fair and consistent way. Instead of just giving a grade like "B," the rubric lets the teacher rate specific skills one by one.

### What ASC Stands For:

- **A — Stream (Beginning):** The student is just starting to learn this skill. They need a lot of teacher help. They cannot do it on their own yet.
- **B — Mountain (Developing):** The student is improving. They can do the skill sometimes but still make mistakes or need occasional help.
- **C — Sky (Achieved / Proficient):** The student can do the skill on their own, every time. They are confident and accurate.
- Some rubrics also have **D — Advanced:** The student has mastered the skill and can teach others or do more difficult versions.

### What the Table Looks Like

It is a grid with:
- **Left column:** The skill or criteria being assessed.
- **Middle columns:** One column for each level (Stream, Mountain, Sky).
- **Each cell:** A small empty circle (radio button). You can click only one circle per row. When you click a circle, a dot appears inside it.

### Example — Language Skills Rubric

| Skill | Stream (Beginning) | Mountain (Developing) | Sky (Achieved) |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------|----------------|
| Reads grade-level text fluently | O | O | O |
| Understands what they read | O | O | O |
| Can summarize a story | O | O | O |

### How to Fill an ASC Rubric Table

1. Read the first skill in the left column (e.g., "Reads grade-level text fluently").
2. Think about how well the student performs this skill.
3. Click the circle under the level that matches the student:
   - If the student struggles with reading and needs help with many words → click Stream.
   - If the student can read most words but pauses or stumbles sometimes → click Mountain.
   - If the student reads smoothly without any help → click Sky.
4. Repeat for each skill in the table.
5. You can go back and change your selection later if needed.

## Key Features

### Save Button
- **Where:** At the bottom of the page, below all 30 tabs. You may need to scroll down to see it.
- **What it does:** Saves everything on ALL 30 pages at once. You do not need to save page by page.
- **How to use:** Click the "Save" button. A green message appears at the top of the screen saying "Saved successfully." If a required field is empty, it gets a red border and the save stops. A message says "Please fill all required fields."
- **Warning:** Always click Save before closing the card. If you close the browser tab or window without saving, all your work on all 30 pages will be lost.

### Auto-Fill
- Student information, school information, attendance data, and LMS scores fill automatically.
- The teacher mainly needs to fill assessment fields (rubrics, comments, grades).
- Attendance percentages are calculated by the computer — no manual math needed.

### Drag and Drop Reordering
- Each section has a drag handle (⠿ icon) on the left side.
- Click and hold the icon, move your mouse up or down, then release.
- This changes the order of sections within a page. It does not delete or change any data.

### Editable Labels
- Double-click any field label (the text name above a field).
- A text box appears. Type the new name and press Enter.
- This lets schools customize field names without needing a programmer.
- Example: Change "Teacher Comment" to "Teacher's Observation."

### PDF Preview
- A "Preview PDF" button at the top or bottom of the screen.
- Click it to see a print-ready version of the completed card with all 30 pages.
- You can print the PDF or save it to your computer.
- The PDF shows the school logo, student information, and all assessment data in a clean format.

## Example Scenario — How a Teacher Uses the Preparatory Card

**Example:** Ms. Patel is the class teacher for Class 5. She needs to fill the Holistic Progress Card for a student named Ravi Sharma.

Ms. Patel logs into the school system on her computer. She navigates to the Teacher Cards section and selects the Preparatory Template. She finds Ravi Sharma in the class list and opens his card.

**Page 1:** She sees Ravi's name (Ravi Sharma), school name (Delhi Public School), and all the parent details already filled in. She scrolls to the attendance table. It shows April: 22 working days, Ravi was present 20 days. The attendance percentage shows 90.9% automatically. She types "Was sick with fever" in the reason column. She notices the LMS Integration section shows Ravi's Math test score: "78/100" in a grey box — she did not type it; it came from the LMS automatically.

**Page 2:** She turns the screen to show Ravi. Ravi selects the boy emoji (👦) and picks his age (10) from the dropdown. He types "Doctor" in the dream job box. He adds his friends "Aarav" and "Simran" by clicking the Add Friend button.

**Page 3 (Language Skills):** Ms. Patel reads the reading skills rubric. She thinks Ravi reads well but sometimes struggles with new words. She clicks "Mountain" for reading fluency. For comprehension, Ravi is good — she clicks "Sky." She types a comment: "Ravi reads with good expression. Needs to work on understanding new vocabulary."

**Pages 4 to 30:** She continues through the remaining pages. On the Mathematics page, she sees the LMS has already filled Ravi's test score (78/100). She adds a comment: "Strong in multiplication. Needs practice with word problems." On the Science page, she uploads a photo of Ravi's science project. On the Art page, she selects "Good" from the grade dropdown.

**After finishing all 30 pages:** She scrolls to the bottom and clicks Save. A green message says "Saved successfully." She closes the card and moves to the next student.
